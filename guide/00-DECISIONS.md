# The NOD Guide. Blueprint Decisions

**Source:** `BookBlueprint.docx` (60-page structural template, modeled on
*Negotiator* / Combalbert & Mery, with the Black Swan parallel-grid device).
**Brand:** Negotiators on Demand. The consumer/practice side, not
negotiatorcruz.
**Relationship to the 33-chapter manual:** deliberately complementary, not
competitive. The manual teaches what you run **inside a conversation** (Read →
Disarm → Steer → Close). This guide covers the **deal arc around it**, before
contact, in the room, after. The Protocol appears inside this book as Step 5,
which cross-sells the manual instead of cannibalizing it.

The blueprint ends with five decisions that block everything downstream. Here
is where each stands.

## The typology decision (Chapter 4's grid)

The blueprint's parallel grid assumes personality types (Analyst /
Accommodator / Assertive). **NOD's doctrine explicitly rejects fixed types**, Ch 5 of the manual: "states are weather, not climate."

So the grid gets adapted rather than copied: the three columns are the three
**states**, **Torn, Ready, Not Yet**, rendered in the identical ten-field
schema. This is honest to the method, it's differentiated from Black Swan
rather than derivative of it, and the states each come with 11+ shipped lines
for the "Tools to use" field. One field is renamed to fit: "Worst-type match"
becomes **"Worst move you can make"**, because against a state, the
antagonist isn't another person, it's the wrong response.

The grid says this difference out loud in its header note. It's a selling
point, not a workaround: types tell you who someone is forever. States tell
you what to do in the next ten minutes, and they change while you watch, which is why the tools work.
