# The Parallel Grid. Three States, One Schema

*Chapter 4's centerfold. Per the blueprint: identical fields, identical order,
no comparative prose. The reader compares by reading across. One page per
state in print. Side by side here for review.*

> **Why states, not types.** Most typologies tell you who someone is forever.
> This one tells you what to do in the next ten minutes. People are not
> Analysts or Assertives all day. They move between three states, sometimes
> inside one meeting, and the state in front of you right now is the only one
> you can negotiate with. Diagnose it, run the tools for it, and watch for the
> shift, because producing the shift is the job.

| **How they see themselves** | Careful, responsible, thorough. | Decisive, clear, done deliberating. | Realistic, unhurried, in control. |
| **How others may read them** | Indecisive, stalling, impossible to close. | Impatient, abrupt, already sold. | Dismissive, complacent, a dead lead. |

| **View of the negotiation** | A decision they're being rushed through. | Logistics standing between them and done. | A conversation someone else scheduled. |
| **Characteristics** | Repeats both sides of the argument unprompted · asks for more information they don't use · timeline slips without hostility · "let me think about it" is sincere | Asks about start dates, not features · corrects your caution ("we're past that") · short answers, forward tilt · introduces implementation words first | Warm, agreeable, zero follow-through · no internal urgency words · meetings reschedule easily · nothing you say changes their week |
| **Reciprocity, giving** | Concessions offered to delay the decision, not to advance it. | Fast, generous on small terms, speed is worth more to them than margin. | Freely agreeable, because agreement costs nothing when nothing will happen. |
| **Reciprocity, receiving** | More options make it worse. They need permission, not inventory. | Receives caution as distrust. Match their pace or lose standing. | Receives urgency as sales pressure. It confirms the "not yet." |
| **Mindset** | Time = safety. Silence = doubt building. | Time = waste. Silence = something's wrong. | Time = abundant. Silence = relief. |
| **Tools to use** | Name the tension, remove the shame: *"You're not indecisive, you're being honest about the tradeoffs."* · *"It sounds like one part of you already knows, and the other part isn't ready to say it."* | Stop selling, start scheduling: *"Let's put a real date on it. Not soon, not this week, a date."* · *"You're not asking if, you're asking how. Let's answer that."* | Find the future where the problem exists: *"What would have to be true for this to become a priority?"* · *"Is this a someday thing, or a never-really thing?"* |
| **How to get them back** | After you've pushed too hard: *"It seems like there's no wrong answer here, just two different risks, and I made it feel like there was."* | After you've over-explained: *"You were ready ten minutes ago and I kept selling. Where do we sign?"*, say it with a smile. The naming is the repair. | After you've chased: *"I've been treating this as urgent when it isn't, for you, yet. What would need to break first?"* |
| **Worst move you can make** | Adding options or pressure. Both feed the tornness. The state resolves by permission, never by force. | Re-opening the case for the thing they've already decided. Every additional argument is a reason to reconsider. | Manufacturing urgency. A fake clock read by a Not Yet confirms everything they suspected about being sold to. |

### The three formula lines

*(Same grammatical shape across all states. The blueprint's stickiness device,
kept because it works.)*

- **Torn:** Time = safety · Silence = doubt building
- **Ready:** Time = waste · Silence = something's wrong
- **Not Yet:** Time = abundant · Silence = relief

### The state check

One line, printed under the grid: **"Which state? Say it before your next
sentence, out loud if you're alone."** Diagnosis before speech, every
exchange. The self-assessment on page four tells the reader their *own*
default state under pressure, which is the column they misread most.
