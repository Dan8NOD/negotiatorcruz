## Why this book and not another one

The catalog now covers the corporate floor three ways: the manual for the
operator, the guide for the deal arc, the drill book for the room. Nothing
covers the person who negotiates ten times a year instead of ten times a
week, and that person was the original audience. The 1st Edition was written
for them. The rewrite moved the doctrine to the corporate register because
that is where the consulting revenue is, but it left the consumer shelf
empty, and the consumer shelf is where the Saturday sessions recruit from.

This book is the consumer register done right. The 1st Edition's audience,
the Protocol's actual tools, and the same 544 shipped lines. Not a softer
method. The same method in smaller rooms.

The argument of the book is in the title. The stakes change. The room
changes. The words do not. A person who learns to say "It sounds like
there's something underneath this" to their landlord has learned to say it
to a buying committee, and the book walks that exact arc, chapter by
chapter, from the kitchen table to a deal with six zeros on it.

---

## Who it is for

The person who does not think of themselves as a negotiator. They are about
to ask for a raise, dispute a bill, buy a car, calm an argument at home, or
take a job offer. They will read this book the week before one of those
events, not as professional development.

Second person throughout. Home register: contractions, short sentences, no
corporate vocabulary in the first half. The reader should never feel they
picked up a sales manual by mistake.

## The rules, inherited without exception

1. **Never fill a `[NEEDS:]` slot.** Every real story comes from Dan or
   stays an empty flagged box. Dan has five years of Saturday sessions with
   exactly this audience. Those are the cases this book needs, and only he
   has them.
2. **Every script line is a shipped line.** Same source as the manual:
   the 544 lines in `negotiation_labels`. Verbatim, with only the dash and
   slot conventions the manual already uses. If no shipped line fits an
   everyday slot, the slot gets flagged, not paraphrased. A shipped line
   rewritten into a domestic version is an invented line.
3. **No dashes, no semicolons, Chicago plain-speech.** Run
   `book/tools/voicepass.py` on everything.
4. **No new doctrine.** Read, Disarm, Steer, Close, the three states, the
   three leverages, defined word for word as the other books define them.
   X1 audits this across all four manuscripts.

## Decisions taken

1. **One book, not two.** Dan's brief spans relationships, personal
   finance, and business deals. That is one arc, not three books, and the
   arc is the product. Splitting it would produce three thin books that
   each promise less.
2. **The bigger-room page is mandatory in every chapter.** It is what makes
   this a Negotiators on Demand book instead of a self-help book, and it is
   the bridge that sells the manual to the reader who outgrows this one.
3. **Same glossary, same definitions.** Any term the other books define is
   used identically or not at all.
4. **The CTA is the Saturday session.** Same single offer as the guide.
   This audience is who those sessions are for.
5. **Cap near 100 pages.** Twelve chapters at roughly 2,000 words plus
   front and back matter. If a chapter wants to run long, the overflow is a
   candidate for the manual, not for this book.
