## Glossary

The words this book leans on, in one place. Where a term is shared
with the companion volumes, the definition is identical there,
deliberately. A series that defines a word two ways is two methods
wearing one brand.

**Accusation audit.** Saying the worst thing they privately think
about you before they can. The construction is fixed: *"you probably
think," "it might seem like."* You are not agreeing it is true. You
are stating, accurately, that it is available to them. Chapters 3
and 4.

**Anchor.** The first number said out loud, and the reference point
every later number is measured against, including by people who know
that is happening. Test one before you counter it. Chapter 5.

**Asking label.** An observation used instead of a question, when the
question itself would reveal what you are worried about. *"It sounds
like you're comparing this against something else you're weighing."*
Chapters 4 and 8.

**Belief.** The settled conclusion under the feeling under the
position. Named rarely, mostly in yourself. Chapter 12, which ships
only with its flag resolved.

**Bracket.** A range rather than a number. People who will not give
you a figure will give you a range, and the top of a volunteered
range is almost always above the number they would have named.
Chapters 5 and 8.

**Calibrated question.** An open question, built on *how* or *what*,
that hands the problem to the other side. Never *why*, which is heard
as an accusation. *"How am I supposed to do that?"* Chapters 6 and 7.

**Currency.** Anything tradeable that is not money: time, scope,
references, start dates, help. Cheap to give and expensive to receive
is the combination that makes a deal. Chapters 5, 7, and 9.

**Encourager.** The smallest listening tool. *"Go on."* Two syllables
and the floor handed straight back. Chapter 2.

**Label.** A short declarative sentence that names the other side's
emotional state out loud, offered without a question mark and without
a defense. Chapter 1, and underneath every chapter after it.

**Leverage, three kinds.** *Positive*, you have something they want.
*Negative*, you can cost them something they fear losing, usually the
strongest and best left unsaid. *Normative*, their own stated
standards held against an inconsistency in their position. Chapter 7.
Note what the pledge does to the second one at a kitchen table: a cost
the other person works out for themselves is a fact, and the same cost
spelled out by you is a threat.

**Mirror.** Their last few words, repeated with your tone going up,
followed by nothing. Decompresses whatever was packed inside the
word. Chapter 2.

**Protocol, the.** Read, Disarm, Steer, Close. The four stages you
run inside a conversation, in that order, every time. This book
teaches its tools in home clothes. The companion manual teaches the
full sequence.

**Silence, dynamic.** The deliberate quiet after a label, a number,
or a question. Whoever speaks next usually gives up ground. Chapters
2, 5, and 8.

**State.** Torn, Ready, or Not Yet. What someone is right now rather
than who they are permanently. States change inside a single meeting,
and producing that change is the job. Chapter 10, which is also where
this book draws the line between producing a change and manufacturing
one.

**Sweep.** The pre-signature question that invites the ask they were
holding. *"What's the thing you almost brought up but didn't?"*
Cheap now, expensive later. Chapter 11.

**"That's right."** The one reliable green light. Distinct from
*"you're right,"* which is appeasement and the sound of someone
ending a conversation. Chapters 1 and 11.

**Trade.** A concession with something named in the same breath as
coming back. Nothing leaves your side of the table for free, because
a free concession teaches them that pressure works. Chapters 5 and 7.

**Walkaway / exit.** What you do if this deal does not happen. It
does not have to be good. It has to exist and you have to have priced
it, because desperation is the one leak no technique closes. Chapters
5 and 8.
