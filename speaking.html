The Problem With Most Keynotes

Your audience has already been inspired.

They've sat through the hero's-journey talk. They've done the standing ovation. They clapped, they felt something real, and three weeks later nobody changed how they open a call.

That's not the speaker's fault — it's a format problem. Sixty minutes of narrative can move a room, but it can't install a behavior. So I use the hour differently: demonstrate the mechanics live, then send everyone home with the four stages and one drill they can run on Monday.

If your audience leaves quoting me, I failed. If they leave running the Protocol, I didn't.

There's still energy in the room — you don't hold 300 people with a diagram. But the energy is a delivery mechanism, not the product.

Logistics

What I need from you

Room

Handheld or lav mic, and the ability for me to get to a volunteer. No stage required — I'd rather be at floor level.

Tech

HDMI and a screen if you want slides. I can run the whole thing without them, and often do.

Travel

Based in Chicago. Domestic travel billed at cost. Virtual keynotes available at a reduced fee.

Recording

Record it, clip it, post it — no restrictions. I'll usually ask for a copy.

Prep

One 20-minute call beforehand so the demo uses your industry's objections instead of generic ones.

Volunteer

Don't pre-select one. The demo only lands if the room knows it wasn't rehearsed.

Tell me about the room.

Audience, date, and what you need them doing differently afterward. I'll tell you honestly whether a keynote is the right format or whether you should spend the budget on a training day instead.
