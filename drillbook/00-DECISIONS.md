# The Drill Book: design decisions

**Working title:** *The Drill Book: 40 Ways to Practice Negotiation Without
Losing a Deal*
**Brand:** Negotiators on Demand
**Status:** third book, scaffolded from `OVERNIGHT-QUEUE.md` item N1.

---

## Why this book and not another one

Both existing books make the same argument on their own first pages: you
cannot learn this by reading. The manual says it in Chapter 3, the guide says
it in thesis two.

That argument creates an obligation. A pair of books that tell you reading
will not teach you the skill, and then offer you only reading, have a hole in
them. This book is the hole.

It is also the only new title currently justified on commercial grounds. It
is:

- **the training-day workbook**, which the one-day and multi-day engagements
  currently have no printed artifact for;
- **the thing a manager runs without Dan in the room**, which is the entire
  premise of the certification tier that does not exist yet;
- **the Saturday session curriculum in print**, which is five years of
  material currently living nowhere.

Every other candidate is a repackage. This one is the missing half of a
promise already made in print twice.

---

## Who it is for

Not the reader of the other two books. **The person running the room.**

That single decision drives everything else about the format. A drill written
for a participant reads as an exercise. A drill written for a facilitator
needs setup time, group size, what to say to open it, what the room will do
wrong, what to score, and what to do when it goes flat. Those are different
documents, and almost every published exercise book gets this wrong by
writing for the participant and leaving the facilitator to improvise.

Assume the reader is a sales manager with fifteen minutes, a conference room,
six tired people, and no theatre training.

## The source material

40 drills, all of which already exist in draft:

| Source | Count | State |
|---|---|---|
| The Cruz Protocol, one per chapter | 32 | drafted, 110 to 290 words each |
| Six Before Yes, one per step | 6 | drafted, with worked answers |
| The 30-day rollout, weekly structure | 1 | drafted in manual Ch 31 |
| The Saturday session format | 1 | exists in the 1st Edition back matter |

**Ch 3 has no drill,** deliberately. It is the how-to-use chapter and there is
nothing to drill yet.

**One defect found while collecting them:** Chapters 5 and 6 shipped the same
drill under the same name, "Three Emotions, Three Minutes." Ch 5 trains the
read, Ch 6 was supposed to train the delivery. Fixed by rewriting Ch 6's as
"The Label Round," which drills saying the sentence out loud and correcting it
on the spot. Collecting drills into one place is exactly the process that
surfaces this, and it would never have been visible chapter by chapter.

Each drill gets expanded from its current 110 to 290 words to roughly 500,
because the manual version assumes the surrounding chapter and this book
cannot.

## The format for every drill

Fixed, so a facilitator can find the part they need mid-session without
reading.

```
DRILL NN        NAME
                what it trains, in one line

TIME            15 min          PEOPLE      3 to 8
NEEDS           a laptop, one real recording
STAGE           Read

OPEN IT         The two or three sentences you say to start.
                Verbatim, because the opening sets whether
                people take it seriously.

RUN IT          Numbered steps. Timed where timing matters.

SCORE IT        What the observer writes down. Numbers, not
                impressions.

WHAT GOES       The two or three ways this drill fails in a
WRONG           real room, and what to do when it does.

HARDER          The variation once the room is competent.

DEBRIEF         The one question to ask at the end. Not
                "how did that feel." Something answerable.
```

**WHAT GOES WRONG is the section that makes this book worth money.** Anyone
can write the steps. What a facilitator actually needs at 2pm with a flat room
is to know that this is normal, it happens every time, and here is the move.

## The two indexes

Ordinary contents plus two lookup tables, because nobody opens a drill book at
page one.

**By what is broken.** The real entry point. A manager does not think "I would
like drill 12." They think *my people cave the second a buyer pushes back*.
That index maps symptoms to drills:

> They discount the moment a buyer goes quiet → Drill 08, Drill 21
> Nobody can tell me who the actual decision maker is → Drill 09, Drill 27
> Deals stall for months and nobody kills them → Drill 30
> They talk too much → Drill 04
> They argue with objections instead of naming them → Drill 10, Drill 14

**By what you have.** Time and bodies, the two real constraints:

> Ten minutes, two people → …
> Fifteen minutes, a room → …
> Solo, on your own calls → …
> Thirty minutes, managers only → …

## Structure

| Part | Contents | Drills |
|---|---|---|
| **How to run a drill** | The facilitator method. Six pages, and the most reused part of the book. | — |
| **Part I: Solo** | Drills that need one person and their own recordings. | ~6 |
| **Part II: Pairs** | Two people, fifteen minutes, no observer. | ~12 |
| **Part III: The room** | Three or more, with an observer scoring. | ~16 |
| **Part IV: Live fire** | Run against real calls and real pipeline. | ~6 |
| **Part V: The programs** | The four-week rollout, the Saturday session format, and a twelve-week curriculum that sequences all forty. | — |

Part V is the commercial centre. A single drill is useful. A **sequence** of
drills with a calendar is a curriculum, and a curriculum is a thing an
organization licenses.

---

## Decisions taken

1. **Facilitator's book, not a participant's.** Settled above.
2. **Forty drills, no padding.** The number is what exists. Do not invent
   drills to reach a rounder figure.
3. **No new negotiation doctrine.** This book teaches no tool the other two do
   not. Every drill points back to its chapter. It is a practice manual, not a
   third methodology.
4. **Reproduction permitted for internal use.** Same reasoning as the
   scorecard: a drill nobody can photocopy is a drill nobody runs.
5. **Cheap and physical.** Spiral bound if print allows, because it gets
   opened flat on a table in a room, not read in a chair.
