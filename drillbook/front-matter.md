## Page 3: This book assumes a room

> This is a facilitator's book. It is written for the person
> running the practice, a sales manager with fifteen minutes, a
> conference room, six tired people, and no theatre training.
>
> If you are practicing alone, start with the solo drills in Part I
> and the two indexes at the front, which will route you by what is
> broken or by what you have. If you are running a floor, read How
> to Run a Drill first, twice, and then run Drill 31 on Monday. The
> rest of the book hangs off that fifteen minutes.
>
> The companion volumes carry the doctrine. This book deliberately
> repeats almost none of it, because a facilitator reading theory
> out loud is the failure state every drill here is built to avoid.

---

## Page 4: The standard

> Drills rehearse tools that work on everyone, including the
> colleague across the table from you in round two.
>
> **The tools practiced in this book are used to create clarity,
> not confusion. More dignity, not less. To listen to understand,
> not to win. When the conversation ends, the other side should not
> feel beaten. They should feel heard. That is the standard.**
>
> In a practice room the standard has one extra clause: score the
> behavior, never the person. The room that mocks its weakest rep
> gets one honest round a quarter. The room that counts behaviors
> gets honest rounds forever.
