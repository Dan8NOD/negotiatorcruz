# Drill 09: Map the Room

*Trains: separating what you know about the buyer's decision structure
from what you assume.*

```
TIME    25 min          PEOPLE    3 to 8, with live deals
NEEDS   a whiteboard, two marker colours, a pipeline review on the calendar
STAGE   Read            PART      The room
SOURCE  The Cruz Protocol, Ch 9
```

## Open it

> "Twenty-five minutes. This trains mapping who actually decides, which
> is different from who you talk to. You'll draw the buyer's org from
> memory, then mark every box with how you know. Green for told
> directly, red for assumed. The reds are why we're here. Go."

## Run it

1. Each rep picks one live deal and draws the buyer's decision structure
   on the whiteboard **from memory.** Boxes for people, lines for
   reporting, a star on whoever can actually say yes.
2. Then the hard part. **Mark every box with how you know.** Told
   directly, one colour. Inferred from something said, second colour.
   Assumed because that is how these companies usually work, circle it
   in red. The colour coding is the drill.
3. Count the red boxes. Count the empty space where boxes should be.
   Say both numbers out loud.
4. Each rep writes the **one sentence** they will use on their next call
   to convert their largest assumption into a fact, verbatim, from the
   shipped families. An information label: "Looks like this decision
   depends on something we haven't talked about." A power label: "It
   sounds like whoever's really in charge isn't even in this room." Or a
   deliberate mislabel: "It looks like this decision is entirely yours
   to make," said exactly so it gets corrected.
5. Next week's review **opens** by checking what came back. The loop is
   the training. A map that never gets tested is a drawing.

## Score it

Per deal: facts versus assumptions, as counted on the wall. Per rep, the
following week: did the written sentence get said on a real call, and
what did it convert? Binary on both.

## What goes wrong

**Confident fiction.** A rep produces a beautiful org chart with two
facts and six assumptions and the room nods at it. That is exactly the
failure the colours exist to catch. If the chart looks finished, ask for
the evidence box by box, and watch it become mostly red in real time.

**The sentence never gets written.** The room agrees assumptions are
bad and moves on. Do not let step four become a discussion. Verbatim,
on paper, before anyone leaves, or next week's opening check has
nothing to check.

**Titles instead of names.** "Their CFO" in a box is an assumption
wearing a lanyard. If the rep cannot name the person, the box is red.

## Harder

Swap deals. Each rep maps a colleague's deal from what the CRM record
alone supports. The gap between the owner's map and the CRM map is the
floor's documentation debt, made visible in one round.

## Debrief

One question: "Which red box, if it stays red until pricing, costs you
the deal?" One box, one name, per rep.
