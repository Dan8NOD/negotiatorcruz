# Drill 24: Two Frames

*Trains: reframing a stuck deal with the same facts, and keeping the
elevation honest.*

```
TIME    20 min          PEOPLE    2 per pair
NEEDS   paper, one stuck objection per person from a real deal
STAGE   Steer           PART      Pairs
SOURCE  The Cruz Protocol, Ch 24
```

## Open it

> "Twenty minutes, pairs. This trains reframing, which is choosing
> which true facts carry the weight. You'll write the same deal two
> ways, both true, and deliver the second one. If any sentence needs
> a fact that isn't real, it doesn't get written. Go."

## Run it

1. The counterpart states a stuck objection from a real deal.
   Something that has been argued at least twice with no movement.
2. The operator writes **two descriptions of the same deal.** One
   framed as today's cost. One framed as twelve months out. Same
   facts, both true. Writing both is the drill: it proves reframing
   is selection, not invention. The facts do not change between the
   two descriptions. Only which ones are load-bearing.
3. The operator delivers the second frame as a vision line from the
   shipped set, "It sounds like you're protecting a future version
   of this, not just today," or "What needs to be true a year from
   now for this to feel like a win?", and holds silence.
4. Second pass: the operator adds one elevation label, "It looks
   like you've earned the right to be particular about who you work
   with," and the room scores only one thing: **is it true?**
   Anything the operator would not say about this person to a
   colleague is struck.
5. Swap.

## Score it

Two binaries per round. Did the second frame use only facts from the
first? And did the elevation survive the truth test? Then the
counterpart reports whether the twelve-month frame changed how the
objection felt, yes or no. No argument about why. The yes count is
the drill's product.

## What goes wrong

**Frame two smuggles in new facts.** A rosier adoption number, an
assumed exec sponsor. The pair checks frame two against frame one
line by line, and anything unsupported gets struck in front of both
of them. Reps who see the strike once stop worrying that reframing
is dishonest, which is the objection that otherwise keeps them from
using it.

**The elevation goes to flattery.** "You're clearly the smartest
person at your company" fails the colleague test and the counterpart
knows it before the sentence ends. The shipped elevation lines point
at earned things: judgment, experience, standards. Stay on them.

**The vision line gets delivered as a pitch.** Rising energy,
leaning in, selling the future. It is a label, and labels are said
like the weather. Flat delivery, then the silence from step three,
uncut.

## Harder

The operator must reframe an objection aimed at their own product's
genuine weakness, without denying the weakness. Holding the flaw and
the frame at once, both true, is the version that separates
reframing from spin, and it is the one the field pays for.

## Debrief

One question: "Which fact turned out to be load-bearing in frame
two?" It is almost never the fact the operator expected, and the
room learns the deal's real center of gravity by hearing everyone's
answer.
