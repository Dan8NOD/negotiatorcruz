# Drill 27: Argue Against Yourself

*Trains: finding the arguments that cannot travel to the meeting you
are not in.*

```
TIME    25 min          PEOPLE    3 to 8, with live deals
NEEDS   paper, one committee-stage deal per rep
STAGE   Read            PART      The room
SOURCE  The Cruz Protocol, Ch 27
```

## Open it

> "Twenty-five minutes. Your deal will be decided in a meeting you
> are not invited to, by people reading documents you didn't write.
> So today you write the case against yourself, the one their
> internal skeptic will make, and then we find out how much of your
> answer can travel without you. Go."

## Run it

1. Each rep writes the **strongest case against their own proposal**,
   the version the internal skeptic would present to the committee.
   Price, risk, the incumbent, the do-nothing option. Full strength,
   no straw men.
2. Mark each point: answerable **in writing**, or only answerable
   **in person**, by you, with conviction and a whiteboard.
3. Anything only answerable in person is a **structural risk**,
   because the decision meeting will not have you in it. Each of
   those items either becomes a document this week or it becomes a
   loss. Write which, next to each.
4. Then the delivery question: **who presents your case in that
   room?** A name, or unknown. Unknown is a red flag with a date on
   it.
5. Write the one question to ask this week that converts the unknown
   into a name, in the shipped shapes: "Who else has to sign off
   before any of this is real?" "It seems like this needs a yes from
   someone else in the room first."

## Score it

Per deal: skeptic points total, the in-person count, and documents
committed with owners and dates. The reliable discovery is step
three: most reps find their strongest argument is one they deliver
verbally, with conviction, in a room, and the actual decision
happens somewhere that argument cannot travel. Score the gap
closing week over week.

## What goes wrong

**The skeptic writes softballs.** "Some may find the price premium
notable" is not what their CFO will say. Same fix as The Five Worst
Things: rank by damage, and require at least one point the rep does
not have a good answer to. That point exists. The committee will
find it whether the rep writes it down or not.

**Documents get promised and not made.** Step three produces a list
and the week produces nothing. Owners and dates on the board, and
next session opens with the artifacts, not the intentions.

**The room argues with the skeptic.** The drill is not a debate
against the imagined critic. It is an inventory of what survives
your absence. Rebuttals delivered out loud in this room prove the
point being scored against them.

## Harder

Swap papers. Another rep, playing the committee, reads only your
documents and decides, out loud, with no rebuttal permitted. Watching
a colleague lose your deal with your own paperwork is the most
persuasive twenty minutes in Part V.

## Debrief

One question: "Which of your arguments dies if you are not in the
room?" Every rep names one. That argument's document is this week's
real work.
