# Drill 39: The Clause That Breaks in a Month

*Trains: finding the undefined word in a done deal, and running the
last negotiation before signature.*

```
TIME    20 min          PEOPLE    2 to 8
NEEDS   one real agreement per pair, signed or nearly, in its actual
        wording rather than from memory
STAGE   Close           PART      The room
SOURCE  Six Before Yes, Step 6
```

## Open it

> "Twenty minutes. A services deal signs Friday, everyone pleased.
> Payment: half on signature, half on completion. Delivery: sixty
> days. And agreed verbally, with warmth: 'of course we can iterate
> until you're happy.' Something in that deal breaks inside a
> month. Find the clause, then find it in your own deal. Go."

## Run it

1. Present the specimen deal above. Each person writes, silently,
   which clause breaks and why. Two minutes.
2. Reveal around the room. The break is the collision between "on
   completion" and "until you're happy," because **completion now
   has two owners and no definition.** Day sixty arrives, the
   assets are delivered, the client wants one more round, and both
   sides are right, which is the expensive kind of disagreement.
3. Now the real work: each pair takes their **own** live agreement,
   with the actual wording in front of them, and hunts the same
   animal. The word with two owners. Done, complete, approved,
   satisfactory, reasonable, promptly.
4. For each one found, write the two-minute conversation that
   defines it, in the shipped shape: "How do we make sure 'done'
   means the same thing to both of us, how many rounds, and what
   happens if we want more?"
5. Anyone whose deal has not signed yet commits to having that
   conversation before it does. Anyone whose deal has signed
   schedules it anyway, because the clause is cheaper to define
   before it breaks than after.

## Score it

Undefined words found per agreement, and conversations actually
had by next session. The specimen teaches the pattern in ten
minutes. The score that matters is the reader's own paper, because
every floor's contracts carry the same animal under different
names, and finding it in the wild is the skill.

## What goes wrong

**The hunt goes legal.** The room starts redlining indemnity
clauses. Wrong altitude. The breaks this drill hunts live in the
friendly words, the ones agreed with warmth, because warmth is
what keeps anyone from defining them. If it was said with a smile,
check it twice.

**The defining conversation gets drafted as a contract clause.**
It is a conversation, two minutes, mildly awkward, run before
signature. Papering it without saying it out loud skips the
actual negotiation and leaves the warmth ambushed later anyway.

**Nobody brings real wording.** From memory, every agreement
sounds fine. The requirement for the actual text is absolute,
because the undefined word is invisible in paraphrase, which is
exactly how it survived the first time.

## Harder

Run it on a deal that already broke. Post-mortem the clause, find
the moment the two-minute conversation should have happened, and
name what it would have cost then versus what the break cost. The
ratio is usually two minutes against two months, and the room
should do that arithmetic on its own deal, not the specimen.

## Debrief

One question: "Which friendly sentence in your current deal has
two owners?" The lock is not paperwork after the win. It is the
last negotiation of the deal, and skipping it un-wins the room
work retroactively.
