# Drill 26: Write Their Review

*Trains: seeing a renewal from inside the client's building, ninety
days early.*

```
TIME    30 min          PEOPLE    each rep alone, then the room
NEEDS   one renewal within ninety days, the account history open
STAGE   Read            PART      Live fire
SOURCE  The Cruz Protocol, Ch 26
```

## Open it

> "Thirty minutes, and it only counts if your renewal is inside
> ninety days. You're going to write the internal review the client
> would write about you, in their voice, criticism included. Then
> we sort every line by how you know it. The lines you can't source
> are this quarter's call plan. Go."

## Run it

1. The rep writes the **internal review the client would write about
   you**, in the client's voice, one page, including the criticism.
   Written in first person as the client. The voice matters, because
   it forces the rep out of their own defense.
2. Mark each line **known** (they have said it), **suspected**
   (inferred, never confirmed), or **invented** (it appeared while
   writing, source unknown).
3. Every suspected line becomes a question for the next call, written
   verbatim, in the shipped shapes: "What haven't you told me yet
   that you think I should know?" "If this ever starts to feel
   one-sided, that's on me to fix, not you to absorb."
4. List everyone on the client side who would be **consulted** on the
   renewal, and mark who has spoken to you in the last ninety days.
   The gap between those two lists is the risk, in names.
5. Name which of the three leverages you actually hold, positive,
   negative, or normative. If the answer is only positive, the
   renewal is weaker than the forecast says, and it goes on the board
   that way.

## Score it

Per rep: the known-to-suspected ratio on the review, the consulted
list gap in names, and the leverage call. Ninety days later, score
the only version that matters: which suspected lines turned out true,
and whether the questions got asked before the client said them
first.

## What goes wrong

**The review comes out as a testimonial.** All strengths, no
criticism, because writing the client's complaints feels like
conceding them. Require at least three critical lines. The client's
real review will have them, and the only question is who reads them
first.

**Everything gets marked known.** "They basically said it" is
suspected, not known. Known means a person, a call, a sentence. The
Map the Room evidence rule from Drill 09 applies unchanged.

**The consulted list stops at the champion.** Renewals die in rooms
the champion does not control. If the list has fewer than four
names, the list is the finding, not the review.

## Harder

Trade reviews. Another rep reads your client's review cold and
predicts the renewal outcome and the discount pressure. An outsider
reading only the client's voice often calls the account more
accurately than its owner, and the floor should see that happen
once.

## Debrief

One question: "Which line of the review do you least want to be
true?" That line is the call to make this week, not at day eighty.
