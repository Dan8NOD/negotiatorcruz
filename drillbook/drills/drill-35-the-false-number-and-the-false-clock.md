# Drill 35: The False Number and the False Clock

*Trains: separating a stated number and deadline into what each is
standing in for.*

```
TIME    15 min          PEOPLE    2 per pair
NEEDS   one real opening demand per counterpart
STAGE   Read            PART      Pairs
SOURCE  Six Before Yes, Step 2
```

## Open it

> "Fifteen minutes, pairs. Your counterpart opens with a number and
> a deadline in one breath: 'it's ninety thousand, and we need your
> answer by Friday.' Both are soft, and you may not argue with
> either one. Probe the number, test the clock, in that order. Go."

## Run it

1. The counterpart opens with a hard number and a hard deadline
   from a real matter, delivered together, with conviction. They
   privately decide what each is actually standing in for: last
   year's spend, a boss's review, a quarter closing, a colleague's
   promise.
2. The operator may not counter, concede, or challenge. The number
   gets the probe: "How did you land on that number?" or "Where did
   that number actually come from?" The clock gets the mechanism
   test: "What happens the day after the deadline that's so
   different from the day before?"
3. Order matters and is scored: **number first, clock second.** Run
   gently, they separate. Argued with, they fuse into a position
   and harden.
4. The counterpart answers honestly from their private backstory.
   The round ends when both origins are on the table, or at four
   minutes, whichever comes first.
5. Reveal the backstory. Compare what the probes surfaced against
   what was actually there. Swap.

## Score it

Two binaries and a time. Did the number's origin surface, did the
clock's mechanism surface, and how many minutes it took. The
teaching number across the room: how many of the deadlines turned
out to be someone else's pressure passed through. A hard number
stated first is usually armor, and armor tells you where someone
expects to be hit. The room should hear that sentence with their
own examples attached.

## What goes wrong

**The probe arrives as a counter.** "Ninety is way off market"
ends the drill. The operator is not disputing the number. They are
asking it to explain itself, and the tone difference is the entire
skill. Rerun the line flat.

**The operator tests the clock first.** It feels safer, and it
lets the number sit unexamined while goodwill gets spent on the
smaller prize. The order rule exists because the number anchors
everything that follows it, so its origin has to surface first.

**The counterpart's backstory is thin.** "It's just the number"
gives the probes nothing to find. The backstory needs a source,
because real numbers always have one, even when the source is
"the last deal that went badly."

## Harder

The counterpart escalates when probed: "Are you saying we're
lying?" The operator must hold the frame, a make-sense line and
the same probe again, without apologizing for asking. Probing
under offense is the field version, and it is where most people
retreat to the counter-offer.

## Debrief

One question: "What was the Friday actually protecting?" The
answers, a status meeting, a boss, a bonus date, teach the room
that clocks have owners, and that the owner is almost never the
person who said Friday.
