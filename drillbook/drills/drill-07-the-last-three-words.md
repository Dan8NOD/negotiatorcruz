# Drill 07: The Last Three Words

*Trains: mirroring past the point of comfort, to find your natural ceiling.*

```
TIME    10 min          PEOPLE    2, in pairs
NEEDS   a timer
STAGE   Read            PART      Pairs
SOURCE  The Cruz Protocol, Ch 7
```

## Open it

> "Ten minutes, pairs. This trains the mirror, which is repeating their
> last few words with your tone going up, and nothing else. Fair warning:
> you will feel ridiculous, and the drill only works if you push past
> that. Counterparts, real work problem, two minutes. Operators, mirrors
> only, at least four of them. Go."

## Run it

1. Pairs. The counterpart talks about a real work problem for two
   minutes.
2. The operator's **only** permitted contributions are mirrors. Repeat
   the last one to three words, inflection up, then stop. No encouragers,
   no questions, not even "mm-hmm." The starvation is deliberate. It
   forces the mirror to carry the whole conversation.
3. A timer runs. The operator must mirror **at least four times** in the
   two minutes, which is deliberately too many. That excess is the
   lesson, not a flaw in the drill.
4. Swap roles and run it again.
5. Discuss one thing only: **at which point did it start to feel like a
   technique?** Everyone's answer lands between the third and the fourth
   mirror. That is the natural ceiling. In live work, halve it.

## Score it

Mirrors delivered, out of four. Second count, from the counterpart: how
many mirrors pulled a longer, more specific answer out of them, and how
many just bounced. The ratio is the skill, and it improves faster than
anyone expects once the inflection gets fixed.

## What goes wrong

**Flat delivery.** A mirror with the tone going down is a statement, and
it sounds like disagreement. The counterpart stiffens instead of
expanding. Fix it in the room by having the operator say the same mirror
twice, once flat, once up, and let the counterpart report the difference.
Hearing yourself do it once fixes it for good.

**The operator adds a tail.** "A budget issue, huh, tell me more." The
tail spends the mirror. The rule is the mirror ends and then it is the
counterpart's turn, however long that takes.

**The laughter takes over.** Some laughter is useful. It means people are
past their self-consciousness. If a pair dissolves entirely, have the
counterpart switch to a genuinely annoying real problem. Irritation
focuses a room faster than instructions do.

## Harder

The operator may only mirror the single **heaviest** word in each
statement, not the last ones. The loaded word, "impossible,"
"ridiculous," "unfair," handed straight back. That is the version that
gets used in the field, and it is what separates a Level 2 operator from
a Level 1.

## Debrief

One question: "Which mirror got you the longest answer, and what word
was in it?" The room learns that the payoff tracks the word chosen, not
the technique itself.
