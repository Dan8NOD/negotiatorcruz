# Drill 19: Where's It From

*Trains: probing a number's origin instead of countering it.*

```
TIME    20 min          PEOPLE    2 per pair
NEEDS   real numbers from real deals
STAGE   Steer           PART      Pairs
SOURCE  The Cruz Protocol, Ch 19
```

## Open it

> "Twenty minutes, pairs. This trains what you do when a number lands
> on the table: find out where it came from before you answer it.
> Counterparts, state a real number and one line of justification.
> Operators, anchor probes only. No counters, no defending your own
> price. Go."

## Run it

1. The counterpart states a number and a one-line justification. Real
   ones from real deals. "We need it under sixty." "Your competitor
   quoted forty."
2. The operator gets four minutes and may only use anchor probes:
   "Where did that number actually come from?" "Is that the number, or
   just the first one anyone said out loud?" "Is that number based on
   this deal, or the last one that went badly?" "What would have to
   change for that number to move?" No counter-offers. No defending
   their own price.
3. The goal: get the counterpart to say what the number is genuinely
   built on, or to admit it is not built on anything.
4. Then the bracket round. The operator must obtain a **range**
   without naming a figure of their own: "Before we talk exact
   numbers, what range are you even considering?" "What's the low end
   you'd still feel good about?"
5. Swap. At the end, compare across pairs: how many numbers survived
   the probe intact?

## Score it

Per round: did the origin surface, yes or no, and was a range obtained
without the operator naming a figure, yes or no. Room-level, the
number that matters: what fraction of the session's numbers dissolved
under two questions. Say it out loud and write it down. Most rooms
find a third to a half, and that discovery does more for a floor's
confidence than any amount of anchoring theory.

## What goes wrong

**The probe comes out as a challenge.** "Where did THAT come from?"
with an eyebrow is an accusation, and the counterpart defends the
number instead of examining it. Same fix as every delivery drill: run
the line twice, hostile and curious, and let the pair hear the
difference.

**The operator answers their own probe.** They ask where the number
came from, get two seconds of silence, and helpfully suggest "the
budget cycle?" The counterpart takes the exit. Probe, then quiet. The
silence rules from Drill 08 apply here unchanged.

**The justification gets accepted as the origin.** "Procurement set
it" is a courier, not a source. One more probe: "What's the story
behind that specific figure?" A number with a real mechanism behind
it survives that question comfortably. A borrowed one does not.

## Harder

The counterpart is instructed to hold an anchor that genuinely has no
basis and to defend it with confidence. The operator must get to "I
don't know, that's just the number" without the counterpart feeling
cornered. Dissolving a fake constraint while preserving the person is
the field version of this skill.

## Debrief

One question: "Which live number in your pipeline have you never
probed?" Every rep names one deal and the exact probe they will use
this week. The drill converts to revenue on that answer alone.
