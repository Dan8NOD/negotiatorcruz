# Drill 28: Hold the Line at Volume

*Trains: staying in Read and Disarm while someone is loud, without
reaching for a solution.*

```
TIME    20 min          PEOPLE    2 per pair, recorded
NEEDS   a recorder, one real escalated matter per counterpart
STAGE   Disarm          PART      Pairs
SOURCE  The Cruz Protocol, Ch 28
```

## Open it

> "Twenty minutes, pairs, recorded, and this is the hardest drill in
> the book. Counterparts, you have a real grievance and you open
> loud, and you stay loud for two minutes. Operators, Read and
> Disarm tools only. No solutions, no policy, no numbers, for four
> full minutes. We are scoring your volume and your clock. Go."

## Run it

1. The counterpart plays an escalated party with a **real grievance
   from an actual matter.** Not invented. Invented anger is polite
   anger, and polite anger teaches nothing.
2. They open **loud**, and they stay loud for the first two minutes
   regardless of what the operator does. That instruction protects
   the drill from early success.
3. The operator may use only Read and Disarm tools. Labels, mirrors,
   the audit, silence, the empathy lines. "That reaction makes
   complete sense given what's on the line." "It sounds like you're
   in a tough spot." **No solutions, no policy, no numbers.** Four
   minutes minimum before Steer is permitted.
4. Score two things from the tape: did the operator's volume rise to
   meet the room, and how many seconds before they offered
   something.
5. Only after the temperature has visibly dropped may the operator
   move to Steer, and the counterpart decides whether it actually
   dropped, not the operator.

## Score it

Seconds-to-first-offer, per person, on the board. Most people offer
a solution inside ninety seconds. Watching yourself do it on tape,
and watching the counterpart's temperature **rise** in response, is
the lesson, and no explanation substitutes for it. Second number:
volume matching, marked wherever the tape shows the operator's
voice climbing.

## What goes wrong

**The solution sneaks in as a question.** "Would it help if we
reissued the invoice?" is an offer with a question mark on it, and
it stops the clock. Same catch as Shut Up and Score: the room
learns to hear solutions in all their costumes.

**The counterpart de-escalates out of kindness.** They see their
colleague struggling and soften early. Restate the two-minute
instruction and restart. The kindness is real and it is stealing
the drill's value.

**The operator goes silent and stays there.** Silence is a Read
tool, but four minutes of stonewalling is not negotiation, it is
absence. The tools are active: label, audit, validate. The tape
should show the operator working, at low volume, the whole time.

## Harder

Two counterparts, one grievance, interrupting each other. Holding
Read and Disarm against a tag team is claims-desk reality, and it
is the version that separates a trained operator from a calm
personality.

## Debrief

One question: "What did it feel like in the ten seconds before you
wanted to offer something?" Naming the internal pressure is how
operators learn to recognize it arriving on a real call, which is
the only moment the training can actually intervene.
