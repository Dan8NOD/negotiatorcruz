# Drill 21: Ask and Absorb

*Trains: asking the no-oriented close, and surviving the two seconds
after a real no.*

```
TIME    15 min          PEOPLE    2 per pair, recorded
NEEDS   a recorder, closing asks from live deals
STAGE   Close           PART      Pairs
SOURCE  The Cruz Protocol, Ch 21
```

## Open it

> "Fifteen minutes, pairs, recorded. This trains the no-oriented
> close and, more importantly, what you do when the no is real.
> Operators, deliver a closing ask built for a no. Counterparts,
> answer honestly, and some of you have a special instruction. Go."

## Run it

1. The operator delivers a closing ask in no-oriented form, from the
   shipped set or built in its shape: "Is there any reason we can't
   proceed?" "Would it be ridiculous to shake on it?" "Have you ruled
   out other options?" "Should I close your file?"
2. The counterpart's instruction, unknown to the operator: answer
   honestly, and **roughly a third of the time, say the hard
   version.** "Yes, actually, close the file."
3. The operator must **respond without arguing.** Acknowledge it
   cleanly, "Sounds like that's a no," ask one calibrated question,
   "What am I not seeing here?", and stop. No discount, no rescue, no
   speech.
4. Score two things: was the question genuinely no-oriented, and did
   the operator absorb the no without negotiating against it?
5. Swap, reshuffle who holds the hard instruction, and run it again.

## Score it

Both binaries on the board, but weight the second. Anyone can ask a
no-oriented question by Thursday. The skill this drill exists for is
the two seconds after a real no, which is when most reps start
discounting, and the tape will show exactly which second it happens
in.

## What goes wrong

**The instant counter-offer.** "Close the file? What if we did
fifteen percent?" The no touched the rep's forecast and the wallet
opened. Score it failed, play the tape, and point at the timestamp.
The room needs to see how fast the reflex is to believe they have it.

**The ask is no-oriented in grammar only.** "You don't want to miss
this pricing, do you?" is a pressure line in a no costume. The test
from Drill 12 applies: a genuine no answer must be genuinely fine.
If it would not be, rewrite before delivering.

**The absorb goes theatrical.** A wounded pause, a heavy sigh, "okay,
I understand, I just think." Performance of acceptance is a
negotiation tactic, and the counterpart can tell. Flat acknowledgment,
one honest question, silence.

## Harder

After the counterpart's no and the operator's one question, the
counterpart adds nothing. The operator must end the round themselves,
cleanly, without reopening the ask. Closing a conversation without
losing the relationship is the version of this skill that pays for
the drill.

## Debrief

One question: "What did the no actually cost you?" The honest answer
in a drill room is nothing, and saying it out loud is the point. The
fear of the no is almost always larger than the no.
