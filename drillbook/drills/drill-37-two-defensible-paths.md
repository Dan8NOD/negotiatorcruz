# Drill 37: Two Defensible Paths

*Trains: choosing strategy by state and time horizon, and pricing the
path not taken.*

```
TIME    20 min          PEOPLE    4 to 8
NEEDS   one real renewal or deal per pair, paper
STAGE   Before contact  PART      The room
SOURCE  Six Before Yes, Step 4
```

## Open it

> "Twenty minutes. A renewal, mildly in your favor. Path A: open
> strong on price and take the margin. Path B: hold price flat and
> trade the increase for a two-year term and a reference. Both
> price out fine. Pick one for your real deal, and then work out
> what the other one would have cost you. Go."

## Run it

1. Each pair takes a real deal, theirs, and writes both paths for
   it. Path A, the margin now. Path B, the compounding trade. Both
   must be genuinely defensible, or the deal is not ready for this
   drill.
2. Each person picks a path and defends it in one minute, numbers
   allowed.
3. Then the turn the drill exists for: the room asks the two
   questions the numbers cannot answer. **What state is the decider
   in, Torn, Ready, or Not Yet? And what outlasts this deal?**
4. Re-vote after the two questions. Watch the paths flip. A Ready
   decider with a healthy relationship absorbs Path A cleanly. The
   same Path A run at a Torn decider hands them a fresh reason to
   deliberate, stalls the renewal a quarter, and spends
   relationship the next renewal will need.
5. Each person writes the cost of their unchosen path in one
   sentence, and the sentence goes in the deal file.

## Score it

Two checks per pair: did the path choice change after the state and
horizon questions, and can each person state the unchosen path's
cost specifically, not as a platitude? The room-level finding to
say out loud: Path A's margin arrives once, Path B's term and
reference compound, and almost nobody prices the difference until
this drill makes them.

## What goes wrong

**The debate stays in the spreadsheet.** Twenty minutes of margin
arithmetic and the state question never lands. The facilitator
holds the two questions until the numbers are exhausted, then asks
them, because the flip only teaches when the room has already
committed to the arithmetic.

**Path B becomes the pious answer.** The room learns that the
relationship path always wins the drill and starts picking it to
be right. It does not always win. That is the point of the Ready
decider case, and the facilitator should make sure at least one
deal in the room genuinely calls for Path A.

**The unchosen cost gets written as a shrug.** "We'd leave some
margin" is not a cost, it is an accounting entry. The cost has a
when and a who: "the next renewal opens with a Torn decider who
remembers being squeezed."

## Harder

Run it on a deal that closed last quarter, against the path that
was actually taken, with the outcome known. Hindsight scoring of a
real path choice is uncomfortable and unusually honest, and it
turns the drill's lesson into the floor's own history.

## Debrief

One question: "Which of your live deals is running Path A at a
Torn decider right now?" Someone always has one, and naming it in
the room is worth the whole session.
