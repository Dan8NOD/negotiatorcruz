# Drill 20: Real or Atmosphere

*Trains: testing every deadline in the pipeline for an actual
mechanism.*

```
TIME    20 min          PEOPLE    3 to 8, with live pipeline
NEEDS   the pipeline open, paper, honesty
STAGE   Steer           PART      Live fire
SOURCE  The Cruz Protocol, Ch 20
```

## Open it

> "Twenty minutes on live pipeline. This trains deadline hygiene.
> Every deadline in your deals goes on paper, and next to it, the
> mechanism: the specific thing that happens the day after. Not a
> consequence in general. The event. If you can't name one, that's
> the finding. Go."

## Run it

1. Each rep lists **every deadline currently in their pipeline**,
   theirs and the buyers'. All of them, including the ones nobody has
   said out loud in weeks.
2. Next to each, write the **mechanism**: the specific thing that
   happens on the day after. Budget line expires, contract lapses,
   the implementation team rolls off, the exec sponsor changes roles.
   Not "we lose momentum." The event.
3. Mark each deadline **REAL** (mechanism identified), **UNKNOWN**
   (never asked), or **ATMOSPHERE** (no mechanism exists).
4. For every UNKNOWN, write the urgency-check question to be asked
   this week, from the shipped set: "What actually breaks if this
   takes one more week?" "Is there a real consequence to missing this
   date, or just discomfort?" "What happens the day after the
   deadline that's so different from the day before?"
5. Reverse it. For each deal, write the buyer's genuine cost of doing
   nothing, and mark whether you actually know it or are assuming it.

## Score it

Three counts per rep on the board: REAL, UNKNOWN, ATMOSPHERE. The
UNKNOWN column is the drill. Most reps are running two or three deals
against deadlines nobody has ever tested, and they have been shaping
their discounting around them. Next week, the carried score: UNKNOWNs
converted to REAL or ATMOSPHERE by an actual asked question.

## What goes wrong

**Consequences instead of mechanisms.** "It would be bad" fills the
column and proves nothing. The test is a date and an event: what
specifically occurs, and on which day. If the room cannot tell a
mechanism from a mood, do one deadline together on the board first.

**The reps grade their own homework kindly.** A deadline the rep
believes in gets marked REAL without evidence. The rule: REAL
requires the mechanism to have been stated by the buyer, not inferred
by the rep. Inferred goes to UNKNOWN, and UNKNOWN gets asked.

**Step five gets cut for time.** It is the mirror image and it
matters as much: a rep who does not know the buyer's cost of doing
nothing is negotiating against a deadline while blind to the real
clock. If time is short, cut the discussion, not the column.

## Harder

Add your own side's deadlines to the same test. Quarter-end is the
most expensive atmospheric deadline in the building, and marking it
honestly in front of a manager takes more nerve than anything the
buyers supply.

## Debrief

One question: "Which ATMOSPHERE deadline has cost you real margin?"
One deal, one number, per rep who has one. The room hears the price
of urgency nobody tested, and the discounting conversation changes.
