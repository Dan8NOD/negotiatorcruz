# Drill 10: The Five Worst Things

*Trains: finding the accusations worth auditing, and saying them without
defending.*

```
TIME    20 min          PEOPLE    3 to 8
NEEDS   paper, pens, silence for the writing step
STAGE   Disarm          PART      The room
SOURCE  The Cruz Protocol, Ch 10
```

## Open it

> "Twenty minutes. This trains the accusation audit, which is saying the
> worst thing they think about you before they do. Step one is written
> and private, because the honesty happens on paper. Write the five
> worst things the other side privately believes about your offer, your
> company, or you. Nobody reads anyone else's list. Go."

## Run it

1. Each person writes **the five worst things** the other side privately
   believes. About the offer, the company, or them personally. Written,
   not discussed. Give it four quiet minutes and protect the quiet.
2. Rank all five by **how much it would hurt if said at the worst
   possible moment.** Not by likelihood. By damage. This re-ranking is
   where the drill earns its time.
3. Convert the top two into audit lines, verbatim, on the page, in the
   shipped construction: "You probably think I'm just trying to squeeze
   you here." "You might be thinking I'll disappear the second this is
   signed."
4. Deliver both out loud to the room. The room's only job is to catch a
   defense. Any "but," any "the reality is," any explanation stapled to
   the end. One catch, redeliver clean, immediately.
5. The hard round: **deliver them again to someone whose face stays
   completely neutral.** Most people can audit to a friendly audience.
   The field does not supply one.

## Score it

Two numbers. Clean deliveries out of total, and how many people's top
two changed between step one and step two. The second number is the
finding: the reliable discovery is that reps have been ranking by
likelihood, and therefore auditing the wrong things all year.

## What goes wrong

**The lists go polite.** "They might think we're a bit pricey" is a safe
item pretending to be a worst thing. If a list has no item that stings
to write, it is not done. Prompt once: "What would their most cynical
person say in the elevator afterwards?"

**Defending disguised as context.** The deliverer says the line clean,
pauses, then adds "which obviously isn't true." That is a defense with a
delay on it. The room catches it the same as any other.

**The room debates whether the accusations are fair.** Wrong question,
and it burns the clock. The audit does not concede the accusation is
true. It states, accurately, that it is available to them. Put that
sentence on the board before the first delivery.

## Harder

Run it deal-specific the week before any major negotiation, with the
actual account. The five worst things a real committee believes about a
real renewal produce audit lines that get used within days, and that is
the fastest route from drill to field in this book.

## Debrief

One question: "Which item did you almost not write down?" That item is
almost always the one that needed auditing most, and everyone in the
room already knows it.
