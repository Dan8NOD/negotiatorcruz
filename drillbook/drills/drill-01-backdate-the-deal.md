# Drill 01: Backdate the Deal

*Trains: dating a negotiation from the other side's first move, not yours.*

```
TIME    20 min          PEOPLE    3 to 8, with their own pipeline
NEEDS   CRM access, paper, a pipeline review already on the calendar
STAGE   Read            PART      The room
SOURCE  The Cruz Protocol, Ch 1
```

## Open it

> "Twenty minutes. This trains finding out when your deal actually started,
> which is almost never when you think. We're counting one thing: how many
> people inside the buyer's building you can name. Pick three open deals.
> Go."

## Run it

1. Each rep picks **three open deals** from their own pipeline. Open deals,
   not closed ones. Closed deals teach you what you already got right.
2. For each deal they write the date the negotiation **started**. Not first
   contact. The date the other side first formed a position.
3. Next to the date: **the evidence.** A comment on a call, a budget cycle,
   a reorganization, a timing coincidence. No evidence, no date. Give this
   step five full minutes and do not let anyone skip to step four.
4. Each rep lists **everyone inside the buyer's organization with an opinion
   about the deal**, including people they have never met, and marks which
   ones they can actually name.
5. Around the table, one question per rep, the same question every time:
   *what happened between that date and today that you didn't see?*

## Score it

Count named participants per deal, out loud, and write the numbers on the
board. Three or more names is a run deal. Two or fewer is a blind one. The
second number: how many reps put a start date **before** their own first
contact. That is the number that should embarrass the room.

## What goes wrong

**Everyone dates the deal from their own calendar.** First contact, first
demo, first proposal. When most of the board says the deal started the day
the rep showed up, stop and ask one question: "Who inside their building was
talking about this before they met you?" Then run step two again on one
deal, publicly.

**A rep pads the participant list with job titles.** "Probably a CFO,
probably legal." Titles are not names. Score only people they can name, and
say so before step four, not after.

**The room turns it into deal review.** Someone starts strategizing about
deal two. Cut it off. The output of this drill is the number of blanks, not
a plan. Plans come after the room can see how much it cannot see.

## Harder

Run it on one live deal per rep in front of the room, with the CRM record
projected. The gap between what is in the record and what the rep claims to
know is the coaching material for the next month.

## Debrief

One question: "Which blank on your sheet costs the most if it stays blank
until pricing?" Make each rep answer with a name or a date, not a theme.
