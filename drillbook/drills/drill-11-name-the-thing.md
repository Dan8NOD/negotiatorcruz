# Drill 11: Name the Thing

*Trains: surfacing a hidden objection with negative labels and silence,
against polite evasion.*

```
TIME    15 min          PEOPLE    2 per pair
NEEDS   index cards with hidden objections, a recorder
STAGE   Disarm          PART      Pairs
SOURCE  The Cruz Protocol, Ch 11
```

## Open it

> "Fifteen minutes, pairs, recorded. This trains getting an unsaid
> objection said. Counterparts, you'll hold a card with an objection you
> never volunteer, and your job is to be politely unhelpful. Agreeable,
> brief, giving nothing. Operators, negative labels and silence only.
> Four minutes. Go."

## Run it

1. The counterpart draws a card with a specific hidden objection written
   on it. Not shared. Their instruction, and the phrase matters, is to
   be **politely unhelpful.** Warm, agreeable, brief, deflecting.
2. The operator gets four minutes. Permitted tools: negative labels and
   silence. "It seems like there's some hesitation." "It sounds like
   there's a version of this you're not saying out loud." "Seems like
   there's a reason you keep hesitating on this specific point." Then
   the quiet. No questions, no solutions, no encouragers.
3. The operator's goal is to get the card's contents said out loud by
   the counterpart, in the counterpart's own words.
4. Reveal the card. Play back the tape and mark which label moved
   something and which bounced off.
5. Swap and run it again with a fresh card.

## Score it

Binary per round: did the card get said? Then the finer number from the
tape: how many seconds of silence followed each label before the
operator spoke again. Labels followed by three or more seconds move
cards. Labels followed by instant talking do not, and the tape proves it
every time.

## What goes wrong

**The operator interrogates.** Questions leak in because labels feel too
slow. The moment a question mark appears, the counterpart gets to answer
just the question, and polite evasion loves a narrow question. Call it,
restart the clock at the last label.

**The counterpart plays hostile instead of pleasant.** Arms folded,
monosyllables, combat. That is the wrong difficulty. Reps train against
hostility constantly and against warm evasion almost never, and warm
evasion is what the field actually supplies. Re-instruct: smile, agree,
give nothing.

**Cards that are too easy.** "Price is too high" surfaces in a minute.
Write cards with a cost to admitting them: "I was overruled last time
and I'm waiting to be proven right." "My predecessor chose you and I
inherited it." The good cards are the ones a real person would hide.

## Harder

The operator must open with a negative label pointed at themselves,
"Sounds like my business isn't worth it to you," before any label
pointed at the counterpart. Going first at your own expense is what
makes the later labels safe to receive.

## Debrief

One question: "Which label came closest to the card's exact words?" The
closer the label, the faster the reveal, and the room learns that
precision, not persistence, is what moves the unsaid.
