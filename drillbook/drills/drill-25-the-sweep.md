# Drill 25: The Sweep

*Trains: asking for the held-back item at the close, and answering it
with a grant or a trade.*

```
TIME    15 min          PEOPLE    2 per pair
NEEDS   a real deal at or near verbal agreement
STAGE   Close           PART      Pairs
SOURCE  The Cruz Protocol, Ch 25
```

## Open it

> "Fifteen minutes, pairs, and this one is deliberately uncomfortable.
> You've just shaken hands, and now you ask what else they wanted.
> Counterparts, you'll name something you were genuinely holding
> back. Operators, you get exactly two legal responses: grant it or
> trade for it. Go."

## Run it

1. Operator and counterpart run to a verbal agreement on a real deal,
   or pick up from a real one that is about to close.
2. The operator delivers a sweep question from the shipped set and
   **holds six seconds:** "Before we call it done, is there a loose
   end we haven't tied off?" "What's the thing you almost brought up
   but didn't?" "Anything you're quietly hoping gets added before we
   finalize?"
3. The counterpart's instruction: name one thing you were genuinely
   holding back. Not invented. A real one from the real deal.
4. The operator must respond in one of exactly two ways: **grant it,
   or trade for it** using the Currency Map grammar from Drill 18,
   "If I move on that, what moves on your side?" Deflecting,
   deferring, or explaining why it is difficult scores zero.
5. Swap. Then around the room: how many of those held-back items
   would have surfaced later anyway?

## Score it

Per round: sweep delivered with the six-count held, and the response
scored grant, trade, or zero. Then the step-five count on the board.
Most rooms find the majority of held-back items would have appeared
eventually, as friction, a support escalation, a harder renewal, and
the difference is that later they arrive with no goodwill attached.
That count is the argument for the tool.

## What goes wrong

**The operator flinches at their own question.** They ask the sweep
and, before six seconds pass, add "but obviously we're in good
shape." The escape hatch tells the counterpart the question was
ceremonial. Same discipline as Drill 08: the hold is part of the
sentence.

**The response goes to committee.** "Let me take that back to the
team" is a deflection wearing a process costume, and it scores zero.
The drill's authority rule from Drill 23 applies: agree beforehand
what the operator can grant or trade, so the two legal responses are
actually available.

**The counterpart names a deal-breaker.** That is not a sweep item,
and it means the agreement was not real yet. Stop the round, note
that the sweep just did its actual job, finding that out now, and
restart with a smaller held-back item.

## Harder

The counterpart names their item and then goes silent themselves,
offering nothing toward the trade. The operator must construct the
trade proposal alone, from the currencies they mapped, without
sweetening it out of discomfort. Trading into silence is the field
version.

## Debrief

One question: "Would your item have surfaced later, and as what?"
The answers, support ticket, resentment, a competitor's opening, are
the case for asking one more thing while the goodwill is still in
the room.
