# Drill 36: The Messenger With No Mandate

*Trains: confirming missing authority without humiliating anyone,
then arming the messenger.*

```
TIME    20 min          PEOPLE    2 per pair
NEEDS   nothing
STAGE   Read            PART      Pairs
SOURCE  Six Before Yes, Step 3
```

## Open it

> "Twenty minutes, pairs. Three meetings in, everything is warm,
> nothing has moved, and you suspect the person across the table
> cannot say yes. Your job: find out without embarrassing them,
> and then change what you are doing. Watch the pronouns. Go."

## Run it

1. The counterpart plays a warm contact with **no real authority**,
   and will not volunteer it. Their instruction: agree with
   everything, commit to nothing, and speak in "we" and "they"
   with no "I." The pronouns are the fingerprint, and playing
   them accurately is the counterpart's whole job.
2. The operator runs the conversation until they suspect, then
   confirms with one warm observation, the shipped line: **"It
   seems like this needs a yes from someone else in the room
   first."** Flat, kind, no triumph in it.
3. The counterpart responds honestly to the delivery: relief if it
   was kind, because pretending to authority is exhausting, or a
   defensive claim of authority if it carried an edge. Either
   answer beats four more warm meetings, and the counterpart's
   reaction scores the operator's tone.
4. Once confirmed, the operator must **visibly switch jobs**: stop
   selling to the messenger and start arming them. Ask what the
   real decider will ask. Offer the one-page version. Then widen
   the room while protecting the person: "You've been carrying
   this internally. Who else should be in the next conversation
   so you're not the only one defending it?"
5. Swap and run it again.

## Score it

Three checks per round. Did the operator catch the pronouns before
minute four? Did the confirming label land as warmth or as
accusation, scored by the counterpart? And did the operator
actually switch from selling to arming, or keep pitching the same
case at a person who cannot buy it? The third check is the one
that fails most, because the switch requires abandoning a pitch
that was going well with the wrong audience.

## What goes wrong

**The operator confronts instead of confirms.** "Do you actually
have sign-off?" wins the fact and loses the person, and the
messenger who was going to become a coalition partner becomes a
gatekeeper instead. The shipped label exists because it lets both
people keep their dignity while the truth arrives.

**The switch never happens.** Authority confirmed absent, and the
operator keeps selling harder to the same chair, as though
conviction could travel by proxy. It cannot. Chapter 27's finding
applies: the argument that needs you in the room dies in the room
you are not in.

**The counterpart overplays the deception.** Shifty, evasive,
obviously fake. Real messengers are warm, competent, and
genuinely enthusiastic. The polite-evasion rule from Name the
Thing applies here unchanged.

## Harder

The counterpart, once confirmed, asks the operator not to go
around them. The operator must widen the room anyway, with the
messenger's help rather than over their objection, in one
conversation. That is coalition work, and it is the guide's Step 3
at full difficulty.

## Debrief

One question: "What did the pronouns tell you, and when?" The room
learns the fingerprint was audible in the first two minutes of
every round, which means it is audible in every real deal they
are currently running warm and motionless.
