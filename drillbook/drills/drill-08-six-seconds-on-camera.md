# Drill 08: Six Seconds, On Camera

*Trains: holding silence after an ask, and seeing your own tells break it.*

```
TIME    15 min          PEOPLE    3 per group (operator, counterpart, timer)
NEEDS   a phone camera on a stand, pointed at the operator
STAGE   Close           PART      Pairs, with a timer
SOURCE  The Cruz Protocol, Ch 8
```

## Open it

> "Fifteen minutes, threes, and yes, the camera is on. This trains six
> seconds of silence after an ask, which sounds easy and is not. Most of
> this room will break at four. Operators, make any ask, then hold.
> Counterparts, stone. Timers, count silently. Go."

## Run it

1. Threes, recorded. Camera on the operator. People need to see
   themselves, not be told about themselves.
2. The operator makes an ask. Any ask. A price, a date, a commitment.
   Real asks from live deals work best.
3. The operator then holds **six seconds of silence.** The timer counts
   silently and reports out loud afterwards, never during.
4. The counterpart's instruction is to say nothing at all. Let it run to
   six or to the break, whichever comes first.
5. Watch the tape together and hunt the tells: the intake of breath at
   three seconds, the hand starting to move, the eyebrow lift, the "so,
   yeah" that escapes at four and a half.
6. Everyone runs it twice. The second attempt, after seeing the tape, is
   usually two full seconds longer.

## Score it

Seconds held, first attempt and second attempt, per person, on the
board. The gap between the two numbers is the value of the camera. Keep
the numbers. This drill gets rerun monthly and the trend is the point.

## What goes wrong

**The room counts out loud or mouths the numbers.** It turns the hold
into a game and the operator into a performer. Timers count on their
fingers below the table and say the number only when the round ends.

**The operator fills the silence with movement instead of words.**
Shuffling papers, checking the phone, a tour of the coffee cup. Movement
is leakage with the sound off. On the tape review, score fidgeting as a
break, because the counterpart across a real table reads it as one.

**Someone argues the whole premise.** "In real life six seconds would be
weird." Do not debate it. Point the camera. The person who breaks at
four seconds while claiming six is unnatural has made your argument for
you, on tape.

## Harder

The counterpart is instructed to look mildly displeased and say nothing.
Silence against a neutral face is one skill. Silence against a face that
appears to be judging you is a different one, and live negotiation
supplies far more of the second kind.

## Debrief

One question: "What was your tell?" Every person names their own from
the tape, out loud, in one sentence. A named tell gets caught mid-deal.
An unnamed one runs the meeting.
