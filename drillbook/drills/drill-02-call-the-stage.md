# Drill 02: Call the Stage

*Trains: hearing which stage a call is actually in, not which one the rep
intended.*

```
TIME    20 min          PEOPLE    3 to 8
NEEDS   a laptop, one ordinary recorded call from your own pipeline
STAGE   All four        PART      The room
SOURCE  The Cruz Protocol, Ch 2
```

## Open it

> "Twenty minutes. This trains telling the four stages apart at speed, on
> tape, which is different from naming them on a poster. Every thirty
> seconds I pause and you write the stage the rep is actually in. We're
> counting disagreements. Here we go."

## Run it

1. Play a recorded call from your own pipeline. Not a demo, not a best-of.
   An ordinary Tuesday call. The ordinariness is the point.
2. Pause every **thirty seconds.** Everyone writes the stage the rep is
   **actually in**: Read, Disarm, Steer, or Close. Not the stage the rep
   intended. Writing, not calling out, so nobody anchors the room.
3. Around the table on each pause. Where two people heard different stages,
   stop and replay the thirty seconds. **The disagreements are the drill.**
   Two honest listeners hearing two different stages means the rep never
   signaled a transition.
4. At the end, map the whole call on the board as a sequence, for example:
   `READ (40s) → STEER → STEER → STEER → CLOSE → STEER → STEER`
5. Read the map out loud. A forty second Read followed by twenty minutes of
   oscillating Steer is not a skills problem. It is a sequence problem, and
   it is fixable in a day.

## Score it

Two numbers on the board. Percentage of pauses where the room agreed, and
the length of Stage 1 as a share of the call. Under 15 percent Read means
the rep diagnosed on the way past. Track both numbers week over week on the
same rep's calls.

## What goes wrong

**The room scores the rep instead of the call.** It slides into critique
and the rep whose tape it is goes quiet for a week. Say at the open that
the tape could have been anyone's, then prove it next week by using
someone else's.

**Everyone waits to see what the senior person writes.** That is why step
two is written silently. If it keeps happening, collect the slips and read
them out unattributed.

**The map comes out clean and the room declares victory.** A clean map on
one call proves one call. Run three calls from three reps before anyone
concludes the floor has a sequence.

## Harder

Play a call from a rep in the room and have that rep score their own tape
against the room's map. The gap between self-score and room-score is the
most persuasive number in the whole program.

## Debrief

One question: "Which stage was simply missing?" On most untrained floors
the answer is Disarm, and hearing the room say it out loud is worth more
than a month of telling them.
