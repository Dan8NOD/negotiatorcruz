# Drill 18: Currency Map

*Trains: finding the non-cash trades a deal is actually made of.*

```
TIME    25 min          PEOPLE    3 to 8, per deal or per account
NEEDS   a whiteboard, one live account
STAGE   Steer           PART      The room
SOURCE  The Cruz Protocol, Ch 18
```

## Open it

> "Twenty-five minutes on one real account. This trains trading, which
> starts with knowing what you have. Two columns: what we can give,
> what they can give. Eight items minimum each, and money may only
> appear once per column. That rule is the whole drill. Go."

## Run it

1. Draw two columns on the board: **what we can give** and **what they
   can give.**
2. Fill both, at least eight items each, and **money may only appear
   once per column.** The constraint forces the non-cash inventory
   that most teams have never written down: timeline, references,
   case-study rights, payment terms, pilot scope, introductions,
   renewal length, exclusivity, training seats.
3. Score every item twice: what it costs the giver, and what it is
   worth to the receiver. Cheap-to-give, expensive-to-receive items
   are your currency. Mark them.
4. Rank the possible trades by that gap, biggest first.
5. Each rep writes the exact sentence they will use to propose their
   top trade, in the shipped grammar: "If I move on price, what moves
   on your side?" "What could I give you that wouldn't cost you
   anything to accept?" "What's cheap for you that would be valuable
   to me?"

## Score it

Items per column beyond money, and the number of marked currencies
with a real cost-to-value gap. Keep the map with the account plan.
Next quarter, score the only number that matters: trades proposed
from the map versus discounts given without one.

## What goes wrong

**The columns fill with variations of money.** Discount, rebate,
credit, free month. That is one item wearing four coats. Enforce the
once-per-column rule loudly and make the room sit in the silence
until a real non-cash item appears. The silence is where the
inventory work happens.

**Their column stays empty.** Teams know what they can give and have
never asked what the buyer holds. If their side stalls at three
items, that is not a drill failure. It is a discovery gap, and the
next call's agenda just wrote itself.

**Cost and value get scored from your side only.** An item is worth
what the receiver would pay, not what the giver is proud of. A
reference call costs you an hour. To a buyer who needs internal
cover, it can be worth a discount you almost gave in cash.

## Harder

Run the map on a deal that just closed, against what was actually
traded. The gap between the map's best trades and the discount that
got given is the floor's tuition bill, in dollars, on one slide.

## Debrief

One question: "Which chip on this board have we never once offered?"
Most floors find six or seven genuine currencies they have been
sitting on for years because margin was the only instrument anyone
had named.
