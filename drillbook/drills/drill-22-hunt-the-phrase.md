# Drill 22: Hunt the Phrase

*Trains: summarizing at the emotional layer until "that's right" arrives.*

```
TIME    20 min          PEOPLE    2 per pair, recorded
NEEDS   a recorder, one real live negotiation per person
STAGE   Close           PART      Pairs
SOURCE  The Cruz Protocol, Ch 22
```

## Open it

> "Twenty minutes, pairs, recorded. This trains the summary that ends
> with 'that's right,' which is the only reliable green light in the
> method. Counterparts, three minutes on a real negotiation you're in.
> Operators, you get one summary, aimed at the feeling under the
> terms, and then we count. Go."

## Run it

1. The counterpart describes a real negotiation they are in, for three
   minutes.
2. The operator may use encouragers, one focusing label, "It sounds
   like there's one specific thing driving all of this," and then
   **one summary.** The summary must aim at the emotional truth rather
   than the terms, in the shipped shapes: "So if I'm hearing you
   right, this is really about trust." "Tell me if I'm off. The issue
   isn't the deadline, it's being blindsided."
3. The counterpart says only one of three things: **"that's right,"
   "you're right,"** or **"no."** Nothing else. The constraint keeps
   the feedback honest.
4. If it is not "that's right," the operator gets **two more
   attempts** and nothing else. No new questions, no more gathering.
   Re-aim with what they already heard.
5. Swap. Track attempts-to-land per person across a month of sessions.

## Score it

Attempts to land, one to three, or a miss. Room average on the board,
dated. The number drops fast for two weeks and then plateaus at the
real skill level, and the plateau is where coaching starts. Note which
misses drew "you're right," because polite appeasement is the most
common failure and the easiest to mistake for progress.

## What goes wrong

**Summarizing the logistics.** "So you need it by June under forty
thousand" is accurate and lands as "you're right" every time. The
material was in the three minutes. The operator named the wrong layer.
Rule four exists exactly for this: forced re-summarizing with no new
input teaches re-aiming, which is the actual skill.

**The counterpart coaches.** "Warmer, kind of, it's more about my
boss." That converts the drill into charades. Three responses only,
enforced kindly, and the debrief can hold the coaching.

**Three attempts become interrogation.** The operator's second try
opens with "okay so is it more that." A question is not a summary.
Attempts that end in a question mark score as misses.

## Harder

Run it on tape from a real call instead of a live counterpart. The
operator summarizes the client from the recording, and the rep who
owns the call judges which layer got named. Summarizing someone who
is not in the room to help you is the field condition.

## Debrief

One question: "What did the counterpart say in minute one that you
only used in attempt three?" The answer is almost always the emotional
sentence everyone hears and discards, and the room learns to stop
discarding it.
