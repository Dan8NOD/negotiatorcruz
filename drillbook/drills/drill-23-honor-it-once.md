# Drill 23: Honor It Once

*Trains: delivering a fairness invitation and paying it when it gets
used.*

```
TIME    1 week, field   PEOPLE    each rep alone, then the room
NEEDS   one live deal per rep, a log
STAGE   Steer           PART      Live fire
SOURCE  The Cruz Protocol, Ch 23
```

## Open it

> "This one runs in the field over a week. You'll deliver a fairness
> invitation on a real call, verbatim, and here is the hard part: if
> they use it, you honor it, on the spot, without escalating. Next
> week you report what it cost and what changed. Pick your deal
> before you leave the room. Go."

## Run it

1. Each rep picks one live deal and delivers a fairness invitation on
   the next call, **verbatim from the shipped list:** "If anything
   here feels unfair, say so and we'll fix it." "Stop me the moment
   this stops feeling fair to you." "You don't have to accept
   something just because I proposed it."
2. They log the response, word for word as best they can.
3. **If it gets used, they must honor it.** A real adjustment,
   however small, made on the spot without escalation and without
   checking with anyone. The authority to honor it is agreed with
   the manager before the week starts, which is half the drill's
   value on its own.
4. In the following week's session, each rep reports two things: what
   honoring it actually cost, and what changed in the conversation
   after.

## Score it

Three counts on the board: invitations delivered, invitations used,
invitations honored cleanly. The reliable discovery lives in the
reports: the cost of honoring is trivial and the change in the
relationship is not. Until a rep has experienced that once, the tool
stays theoretical, and a fairness invitation nobody believes in is
worse than none.

## What goes wrong

**The invitation gets delivered with an escape hatch.** "Obviously
within reason" stapled to the end converts the invitation into a
slogan. Verbatim means verbatim. The lines were built without
hatches on purpose.

**The rep honors it with a hedge.** "I mean, we could probably look
at that." The counterpart hears the difference between an adjustment
and a stall instantly. If it was invited and requested, it moves,
now, visibly.

**Nobody uses it, and the room calls the drill a dud.** An unused
invitation is not a null result. It got heard, and it compounds. Log
it, and watch what that counterpart does at the next friction point.

## Harder

The room-based version, for teams who need a rehearsal first: the
operator delivers the invitation, the counterpart immediately uses
it on something significant, and the operator must respond without
hedging, escalating, or explaining. Score the hedge. Run it before
the field week if the floor has never said these lines out loud.

## Debrief

One question, next week: "What did honoring it cost, in dollars or
minutes, and what did it buy?" The answers make the argument no
manager can make from the front of the room.
