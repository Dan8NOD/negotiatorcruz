# Drill 04: Shut Up and Score

*Trains: listening without assembling a response, under a stopwatch.*

```
TIME    25 min          PEOPLE    3 per group (operator, counterpart, timer)
NEEDS   a stopwatch per group, one real situation per counterpart
STAGE   Read            PART      Pairs, with a timer
SOURCE  The Cruz Protocol, Ch 4
```

## Open it

> "Twenty-five minutes, groups of three. This trains the least popular skill in
> the building: listening without fixing. Operators, for six minutes you
> may encourage, ask, and repeat. You may not solve. We're counting your
> talk time. Counterparts, bring something real. Go."

## Run it

1. Threes. One operator, one counterpart, one timer. The counterpart brings
   a **real** situation from their own work. Not invented. Invented
   problems are too tidy and have none of the fuzzy words in them.
2. The operator gets **six minutes** under a hard constraint: minimal
   encouragers, Listen-For questions, and Clean Language only. "Go on."
   "Is this a won't, or is it a can't?" "What are you actually trying to
   protect in this?" When the counterpart says "complicated," the operator
   may ask "complicated how?" Nothing that solves. Nothing that starts
   with "I."
3. The timer marks talk time with the stopwatch. Thumb down while the
   operator speaks, thumb up when they stop. The physical thumb matters.
   It makes the reflex visible to the operator mid-sentence.
4. At six minutes the operator states the counterpart's position back in
   one paragraph, accurately enough that the counterpart says **"yeah,
   that's it."** Not kindly. Accurately.
5. Rotate all three seats. Then compare talk-time numbers across groups.

## Score it

Operator talk time as a percentage of the six minutes. Under 25 percent is
the target. Second score, binary: did the counterpart accept the playback
without correcting anything material?

## What goes wrong

**The operator smuggles solutions in as questions.** "Have you considered
just calling their VP?" is a solution wearing a question mark. The timer
calls it, the round continues. Name this failure in the open, because it
is the one everyone commits first.

**The counterpart performs a problem instead of having one.** If the story
sounds rehearsed and resolves neatly, it is not generating the fuzzy words
the drill needs. Swap in a real, current, unresolved situation, even a
small one. Small and real beats big and staged.

**The room mocks the discomfort.** The first round is uncomfortable and
someone will be loud about it. Let them. Then point at the talk-time
numbers. The discomfort is the reflex being noticed for the first time.

## Harder

Cut the operator's window to four minutes and require the playback to
include one thing the counterpart never said directly. That forces reading
underneath the words, which is where Chapter 5 begins.

## Debrief

One question: "How many times did you physically stop yourself from
talking?" The number is the finding. Before this drill, nobody believes
the reflex exists in them.
