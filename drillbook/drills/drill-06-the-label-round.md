# Drill 06: The Label Round

*Trains: saying the label out loud, cleanly, which is a different skill
from knowing what the label is.*

```
TIME    15 min          PEOPLE    3 to 8
NEEDS   a laptop, ninety seconds of one real recorded call
STAGE   Read            PART      The room
SOURCE  The Cruz Protocol, Ch 6
```

## Open it

> "Fifteen minutes. This trains delivery. Plenty of people can name an
> emotion on paper and cannot say it to a person without softening it into
> nothing. You'll each write one label and say it to the room at full
> volume. We're counting three fails: starts with 'I,' ends like a
> question, or anything follows it. Go."

## Run it

1. Play ninety seconds of a real recorded call. The team's own, not a
   demo.
2. Everyone writes **one label**, in full, word for word. Not a
   description of the emotion. The sentence they would actually say, built
   on the shipped stems: "It seems like," "It sounds like," "It looks
   like," "It feels like."
3. Around the table, each person **delivers theirs out loud** to the room,
   at the pace and volume they would use on the call. Reading it is not
   delivering it.
4. The room scores three things per delivery, and only these three:
   did it start with "I," fail. Did it end with a question mark, spoken or
   implied by rising tone, fail. Did anything follow it, fail.
5. **Anyone who fails delivers again immediately**, corrected, before the
   next person goes. The immediate re-delivery is the whole drill. A note
   in a margin teaches nothing. Saying the sentence cleanly ten seconds
   after saying it wrong is what installs it.

## Score it

Clean deliveries out of total deliveries, first attempt. Write the room's
number on the board and date it. It should approach ten out of ten within
four weekly runs, and when it does, switch scoring to specificity: generic
emotion words score zero even in a clean delivery.

## What goes wrong

**The rising tone hides.** People flatten the words and lift the last
half syllable, and the room lets it pass. Appoint one listener whose only
job is the ending. The tone is the most common fail and the least
reported.

**Softeners creep in.** "Maybe it kind of seems like." Every hedge word
is the deliverer asking permission. Strike them in the rewrite and have
the person deliver the stripped version. It will feel too blunt to them
and sound exactly right to everyone else.

**The re-delivery gets skipped when time runs short.** Cut the number of
deliverers before you cut the re-delivery. Half the room drilling
correctly beats all of the room drilling once, wrong.

## Harder

Deliver to a person instructed to look mildly unimpressed and say nothing
back. Most people can label into a friendly face. The field supplies the
other kind.

## Debrief

One question: "What did your own voice do that you didn't plan?" The
answers name the exact tells, the lift, the hedge, the nervous addendum,
and once a person has named their tell they can hear it coming.
