# Drill 38: The Hardened Position, With the Room Watching

*Trains: answering an ultimatum with a probe instead of a counter,
in front of an audience.*

```
TIME    20 min          PEOPLE    5 to 8, one lead plus a watching team
NEEDS   a conference table, or chairs arranged like one
STAGE   Steer           PART      The room
SOURCE  Six Before Yes, Step 5
```

## Open it

> "Twenty minutes, and this one needs the audience, so everyone not
> playing is playing the other side's team. Their lead says:
> 'Ninety or we're done. That's the number.' Operators, you have
> three options, and two of them cost you the next round. Go."

## Run it

1. One person is the counterpart's lead. Three or more others sit
   on their side of the table as the watching team, silent,
   attentive, taking notes. **The audience is the drill's active
   ingredient**, because a lead who has stated a number in front
   of their own people cannot climb down from it.
2. The lead opens flat: "Ninety or we're done. That's the number."
3. The operator responds. The two losing moves get named after the
   round, not before: countering at seventy legitimizes ninety and
   turns the negotiation into arithmetic their number started, and
   restating the value case answers a position with a position,
   making any movement a public loss for the lead.
4. The winning shape is the Protocol in order, and the round is
   scored against it: a read first, "It sounds like this number
   isn't really about this deal," then the probe, "How did you
   land on ninety?", then **six seconds of silence.**
5. The lead responds honestly: if the probe gave them a way to
   move without moving, they explain the number, and the real
   constraint surfaces. If the operator countered or pitched, the
   lead hardens, because their team is watching. Rotate.

## Score it

Three checks per round: no counter-number offered, the probe asked
before any case was made, and the silence held to six. Then the
lead reports the key internal fact: at which moment did backing
down become possible? The answer is always the same shape,
explaining a number is not retreating from it, and the room needs
to hear a lead say it firsthand.

## What goes wrong

**The operator treats the ultimatum as real.** Panic-concessions
inside thirty seconds. The Real or Atmosphere test applies to
ultimatums too, and most "that's the number" numbers have a story
that dissolves them. The probe is how the story arrives.

**The operator treats the ultimatum as fake.** Smirking past it is
as expensive as folding, because the lead's team just watched
their lead get dismissed. The probe works exactly because it
takes the number seriously enough to ask about it.

**The audience breaks role.** Giggling teammates dissolve the
pressure that makes the drill matter. Brief them: you are the
reason your lead cannot back down. Sit like it.

## Harder

Two ultimatums in one round: the number, and a deadline stapled to
it. The operator must probe both, in the right order, number
first, without the audience ever seeing a counter. This is Drill
35 run under stage lights, and it is the guide's Step 5 at full
field pressure.

## Debrief

One question, to the lead first: "What did the probe let you do
that the counter would not have?" Then to the operator: "What did
the silence cost you to hold?" Both answers together are the whole
chapter.
