# Drill 17: Three, In Their Words

*Trains: summarizing in the other side's vocabulary, and catching the
item everyone drops.*

```
TIME    20 min          PEOPLE    2 per pair, recorded
NEEDS   a recorder, one real complicated situation per person
STAGE   Steer           PART      Pairs
SOURCE  The Cruz Protocol, Ch 17
```

## Open it

> "Twenty minutes, pairs, recorded. This trains the summary, which is
> playing back what they said so accurately they'd sign it. The catch:
> their words, not yours. Counterparts, four minutes on something real
> and messy. Operators, encouragers only until it's summary time. Go."

## Run it

1. The counterpart talks about a real, complicated situation for **four
   minutes.** The operator may use encouragers and nothing else.
2. The operator delivers a summary in **three items**, and must use the
   counterpart's own vocabulary. Their nouns, their adjectives, their
   framing. The shipped shape works out loud: "So: you want speed, you
   need cover, and you've been burned before." Then the invitation:
   "Here's everything you've told me. Correct anything I get wrong."
3. The counterpart scores it out of three. One point per item they
   would sign their name to. Not close, sign.
4. The operator then delivers the isolate version: what is agreed,
   what is still open, one sentence each.
5. Swap and run it again.

## Score it

Points out of three, tracked across weeks. The pattern to surface on
the board: reps consistently believe they summarized accurately and
consistently score two, and the missing item is almost always the
emotional one. They capture the logistics and drop the part about
being burned before. Naming that pattern to the room is half the value
of the drill.

## What goes wrong

**Translation.** The counterpart says "we got surprised by an invoice"
and the summary says "billing transparency concerns." It has stopped
being a thing anyone said. The counterpart scores translated items
zero even when the meaning survived, because in the field the meaning
does not survive. People sign their own words and audit yours.

**The summary grows.** Three items become six and the counterpart
loses the shape. Hold the line at three. Choosing what makes the cut
is the skill being trained.

**The operator sneaks a pitch into item three.** "And you're looking
for a partner who can move fast" was not said by anyone. Score it
zero, say why, and watch the room start listening instead of
positioning.

## Harder

Ban the operator from using any word the counterpart did not use.
Harder than it sounds, and it cures the vocabulary-upgrade habit
permanently. Run this variant once the room reliably scores three.

## Debrief

One question: "Which item did you almost leave out, and why?" The
answer is nearly always the feelings item, and the room saying so out
loud is what changes next week's summaries.
