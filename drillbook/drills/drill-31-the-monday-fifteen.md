# Drill 31: The Monday Fifteen

*Trains: the facilitator. The fixed weekly session every other drill
in this book runs inside.*

```
TIME    15 min          PEOPLE    the whole floor, weekly
NEEDS   one tool chosen from the week's stage, a timer
STAGE   All four        PART      The room
SOURCE  The Cruz Protocol, Ch 31
```

## Open it

> "Fifteen minutes, same room, same time, every Monday. The
> structure never changes, only the tool does. Today's tool is on
> the board. Here's the standard, read aloud, and here's me doing
> it badly first. Go."

## Run it

The structure is fixed, and the fixedness is the tool:

1. **Minutes 0 to 2.** Name the tool. Read its standard aloud from
   the manual. No discussion. Discussion is how fifteen minutes
   becomes forty.
2. **Minutes 2 to 5.** The facilitator demonstrates it **badly on
   purpose, then correctly.** The bad version first is not a joke.
   It gives the room permission to be bad at it in the next ten
   minutes, and that permission is the precondition for practice.
3. **Minutes 5 to 12.** Pairs or threes. One negotiator, one
   counterpart, one observer. Three rounds of about two minutes,
   rotate roles each round. The observer's only job is to **count.**
   Not advise. Count.
4. **Minutes 12 to 15.** Observers report numbers only. "She labeled
   twice and both times kept talking." No opinions. Opinions are
   the failure state of practice, and the numbers-only rule is what
   keeps the session safe enough to repeat.

Inside a rollout, the tool follows the four-week calendar: Read
tools in week one, Disarm in week two, Steer in week three, Close in
week four. One tool per week, and the temptation to compress this
into two weeks should be resisted. The constraint is not how fast
people learn a sentence. It is how long a sentence takes to survive
a bad call.

## Score it

The session scores itself: the observer counts are the metric, and
the same count tracked across four Mondays is the floor's actual
learning curve. The facilitator's own score is binary and honest:
did it start on time, and did it end at fifteen.

## What goes wrong

**It becomes a meeting.** Discussion at minute two, a pipeline
question at minute six, and the drill dies of agenda. The
facilitator's authority here is the clock, and using it kindly and
relentlessly is the whole job.

**The demo goes polished.** A facilitator who demonstrates the tool
well first sets a bar the room quietly declines to attempt. Badly
first, always. The laugh is load-bearing.

**Week one gets scored on the weakest rep.** Friday's public scoring
starts with the facilitator's own tape or a volunteer's. Score your
weakest rep in public in week one and you will not get another
volunteer for a month.

**It skips a week.** The Monday Fifteen survives on rhythm. One
skipped Monday costs two, because the second one now needs a reason
where a habit used to be.

## Harder

Hand the facilitation to a rep. The manager observes, counts, and
says nothing. A floor where the Monday Fifteen runs without its
manager is a floor where the install actually took, and that is the
certification argument in one room.

## Debrief

One question, for the facilitator alone, after the room empties:
"Which number moved since last Monday?" If no number moved, next
week runs the same tool again. Repetition is what the method asks
for, and the calendar is where most floors quietly refuse it.
