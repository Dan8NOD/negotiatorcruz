# Drill 05: Three Emotions, Three Minutes

*Trains: naming specific emotions with evidence, and calling the state.*

```
TIME    15 min          PEOPLE    3 to 8
NEEDS   a laptop, three minutes of one real recorded call
STAGE   Read            PART      The room
SOURCE  The Cruz Protocol, Ch 5
```

## Open it

> "Fifteen minutes, no prep. This trains the vocabulary, which is the
> foundation of the whole Read stage. Three minutes of tape, then you each
> write three emotions the counterpart is in, with evidence. Fair warning:
> 'frustrated' scores zero. Rolling."

## Run it

1. Play three minutes of a real recorded call. Everyone silent.
2. Everyone writes **three emotions** they believe the counterpart is in.
   Specific ones. Cornered, exposed, worn down, blindsided. The Chapter 5
   vocabulary is the menu, and the counterpart's own word beats the menu.
3. Next to each emotion, **the evidence.** The exact phrase, the pause that
   ran long, the shift in pace. No evidence, no credit, no matter how right
   the word sounds.
4. Everyone also writes the **state**: Torn, Ready, or Not Yet. One word.
5. Around the table. Two scoring rules, stated before anyone reads:
   *"frustrated" scores zero*, it is the "loud" of emotional vocabulary,
   and any emotion without an evidence line scores zero.
6. Compare the state calls. Where the room splits, replay those thirty
   seconds and argue about it. **The argument is the training.**

## Score it

Per person: emotions that survived both rules, out of three. Per room: did
the state call converge after replay? Track the survival rate weekly. It
climbs fast and then it stalls, and where it stalls is where coaching
starts.

## What goes wrong

**The room writes categories instead of states.** "Concerned about
budget" is a topic, not an emotion. Push one level down: concerned how?
Squeezed? Second-guessed? Waiting for the catch? The vocabulary list
exists so nobody has to generate words under pressure.

**Everyone converges on the same safe read.** Eight people, one emotion,
usually the obvious one. If that keeps happening, ban the first emotion
named and make everyone find two more. The second and third reads are
where the actual skill lives.

**The evidence rule slips.** Halfway through, people start asserting
without quoting. Reinstate it loudly. The rule is the drill. Emotional
guessing without evidence is what the floor was already doing for free.

## Harder

Run it twice on the same call: once audio only, once transcript only. The
gap between what the room hears and what the room reads is where somatic
reading lives, and nobody believes the size of that gap until they watch
it happen to them.

## Debrief

One question: "Which piece of evidence did you hear that nobody else
wrote down?" It rewards the specific listening the drill exists to build.
