# Drill 32: Coach the Coach

*Trains: the managers. Coaching one behavior, rehearsed in the room,
to one commitment.*

```
TIME    30 min          PEOPLE    3 managers, monthly
NEEDS   one real rep recording per manager
STAGE   All four        PART      The room, managers only
SOURCE  The Cruz Protocol, Ch 32
```

## Open it

> "Thirty minutes, managers only. Reps get drilled every Monday.
> Nobody drills us, and it shows. One of you coaches, one of you
> plays your own rep, one of you scores four boxes and nothing
> else. Go."

## Run it

1. Managers pair up, third as observer. One brings a real rep
   recording and coaches the other manager **as though they were
   that rep**, tape queued to the moment worth coaching.
2. The observer scores four things only:
   did the coach ask for the rep's own read **first**, did they name
   **one** behavior rather than several, did they make the rep
   **rehearse the correction in the room**, and did they close on
   **one measurable commitment**.
3. Swap roles and run it again with the second recording.
4. Then the reversal: the observing manager plays a rep who
   **politely resists.** "Yeah, that's fair, I mean, that call was
   kind of an outlier." Score one thing: did the coach name the
   resistance, "It sounds like this feels unfair," or push through
   it with more feedback?
5. Rotate until everyone has coached once and resisted once.

## Score it

The four boxes, tallied per coach, monthly, on the same board the
reps' numbers live on. That placement is deliberate. A floor where
manager scores hang next to rep scores is a floor where the Level
ladder means something, and this drill is what makes internal
certification credible. A manager who cannot pass it cannot sign
anyone off.

## What goes wrong

**The coach delivers a review instead of a rehearsal.** Five
observations, zero reps. The one-behavior rule exists because a rep
can install one correction per session and will nod politely
through five. The observer counts behaviors named and stops the
round at two.

**The rehearsal gets skipped as awkward.** Two managers role-playing
feels silly for ninety seconds. It is also the entire difference
between coaching and commentary, which is the same finding as
every drill in Part I: the sentence has to happen in the mouth.

**The resistance round turns into a debate.** The resisting manager
argues the call's merits and the coach litigates. Wrong game. The
skill being scored is recognizing that the resistance is now the
conversation, and labeling it before returning to the behavior.

## Harder

Coach from a recording of the coach's own call. A manager who can
name their own failed label in front of two peers has earned the
right to name anyone's, and the floor can smell the difference.

## Debrief

One question: "Which of the four boxes do you skip on a busy
Tuesday?" Every manager knows their answer immediately. The box
they skip is the one their whole team is weakest at, and that is
never a coincidence.
