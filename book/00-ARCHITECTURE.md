# The Manual — Rewrite Architecture

**Working title direction:** *The Cruz Protocol: A Field Manual for Commercial Negotiation*
**Source:** Dan Cruz / Negotiators on Demand (Kindle edition) — PDF pending
**Job of this book:** raise the price of the author.

---

## 1. What we are actually building

This is not a book project with a consulting business attached. It is a
consulting business with a book as its credentialing instrument.

A dream-based book converts a reader into a fan. A fan buys a $10 Kindle
edition and tells you it changed their life. That is a nice outcome and a
dead end — there is no line item on a corporate budget called "inspiration."

A manual converts a reader into a **buyer with a budget code**. The mechanism
is simple and it is the entire strategy:

> A manual is documentary evidence that a *system* exists.
> A system is the only thing a committee can approve.

An L&D director, a VP of Sales, a procurement lead — none of them can defend
"we're bringing in a motivational speaker" in a budget review. All three can
defend "we're licensing a documented four-stage methodology with a scoring
rubric and a 30-day implementation plan, and the author runs the install."

The book's job is to make the second sentence obviously true before anyone
gets on a call. Everything in the rewrite serves that.

### The rate mechanics

| Book reads as | Buyer's mental category | What they'll pay |
|---|---|---|
| Memoir / dream | Speaker | $2–5K, one-time, no follow-on |
| Tips & tactics | Trainer | $5–8K, commodity, price-shopped |
| **Documented system with rubrics** | **Methodology owner** | **$15–20K install + retainer** |

The current ladder tops out at $15–20K for the multi-day immersive. A manual
that reads as infrastructure supports that number without discounting, and
opens the rung above it that doesn't exist yet: **licensed internal
certification** — where a client's own managers are certified to run the
Protocol, and Dan is paid annually rather than per-event.

Rung 3 is the pivot point of the whole ladder. Right now the site tells
buyers, correctly, "buy this before you buy anything else from me." That
means the book is doing the qualifying *and* the pricing. It has to read like
the $20K thing is the natural next step, not the upsell.

## 2. The seven conversions

Every chapter gets rewritten through these. This list is the actual editing
standard — when a passage is unclear, check it against this table.

| # | From (dream book) | To (manual) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **First person past** — "I walked into that room and everything changed." | **Second person imperative** — "You walk in with one job: name the emotion before you name a number." |
| 2 | **Story as the point** | **Story as evidence, capped.** Every anecdote earns its place by demonstrating one tool, and ends with the mechanism named. Max one per chapter. |
| 3 | **Inspiration** — "You can do this." | **Instruction** — "Here is the sentence. Say it exactly. Here is what a good response sounds like." |
| 4 | **Unfalsifiable** — works if you believe in it | **Falsifiable.** Every chapter names its failure mode and the conditions under which the tool is the wrong choice. Naming what a method *can't* do is the single strongest credibility signal to a corporate buyer. |
| 5 | **Unmeasured** — success is how it felt | **Scored.** Every tool has a metric a manager can audit from a recording. |
| 6 | **Ends with encouragement** | **Ends with an artifact** — a drill, a script card, a scorecard row the reader can put in front of their team Monday. |
| 7 | **Author as hero** | **Reader as operator, author as the person who wrote the standard.** Authority moves from charisma to documentation. This is what survives the reader forgetting your name. |

**One thing we do not throw away.** The site is already honest about this and
the book should be too: energy opens the door. The origin material, the
Marine background, the 1,000+ live sessions — that stays. It moves from being
the *spine* of the book to being its *credentials*, concentrated in the front
matter and in evidence blocks, where it does more work in less space.

## 3. The chapter spine (non-negotiable)

Every one of the 33 chapters runs the same seven-part structure, in this
order. Same sequence, every chapter — this is the book making the same
argument in its form that the Protocol makes in its content.

```
1. THE STANDARD      One paragraph. What this tool is and what it is for.
                     No throat-clearing, no story opener.

2. WHEN TO REACH FOR IT
                     Trigger conditions. "Use this when ___." And the
                     honest inverse: "Do not use this when ___."

3. THE WORDS         Verbatim scripts in a box. Three to five variants
                     for different buyer temperaments. Word-for-word,
                     because that is what gets used.

4. WHAT GOOD SOUNDS LIKE
                     The response you're listening for, and how to read a
                     response that isn't it.

5. THE DRILL         A rehearsal a manager can run in under 15 minutes
                     with no preparation. Timed. Recorded where possible.

6. SCORED ON         The metric. Specific enough to audit from a tape.
                     (e.g. talk-time ratio under 40%; zero concessions
                     after the other side goes quiet.)

7. FAILURE MODE      How this tool goes wrong in the field, what it looks
                     like when it does, and the recovery.
```

Plus a **one-page Field Card** at the end of every chapter — the whole
chapter compressed to what fits on a laminated card. Those 33 cards are a
standalone deliverable: the leave-behind for the training day, and the
strongest single artifact in the whole funnel.

## 4. The 33 chapters

Proposed re-architecture. Six parts, each mapped to what it's doing in the
funnel. Final tool→chapter assignment gets locked against the existing
31-tool catalog once the PDF lands.

### Part 0 — The Standard (Ch 1–3) · *sells the system*
The most commercially important part of the book. This is what a VP reads
in the Amazon sample before deciding whether to forward it.

1. **Why Protocol Beats Motivation** — the 72-hour decay problem. Opens on
   the day-20 collapse, not on an origin story.
2. **The Four Stages** — Read → Disarm → Steer → Close. The whole method in
   one chapter, including why the order is fixed and what breaks when it isn't.
3. **How to Use This Manual** — operating instructions. Solo reader, team
   lead, and manager-running-a-floor each get a different path through the
   book. This chapter is what makes it a manual instead of a book about a manual.

### Part I — READ (Ch 4–10) · *diagnose before you ask*
Emotion vocabulary, labels, tactical silence, listening under pressure,
reading the room you're not in (phone/email/video), and diagnosing the
decision structure behind the person in front of you.

### Part II — DISARM (Ch 11–16) · *defuse before you steer*
Accusation audit, mirrors, naming your own weakness first, handling the
objection that hasn't been said, and the discipline of not defending.

### Part III — STEER (Ch 17–23) · *move them without pressure*
Calibrated questions, the How/What grammar, summaries, pacing, talk-time
discipline, transferring the problem, and the anatomy of a concession.

### Part IV — CLOSE (Ch 24–28) · *restraint as technique*
No-oriented questions, the "that's right" summary, the six-second hold,
implementation-forward closing, and getting out of the way.

### Part V — Under Pressure (Ch 29–31) · *proves it's a real system*
Where most training books stop and where corporate credibility is actually
won. Multi-party and committee negotiations. Negotiating internally — up,
down, and sideways. **And a full chapter on when the Protocol fails**: bad
faith, no-authority counterparts, genuine deadlock, and when to walk. A
method with published limits reads as engineering. A method without them
reads as a pitch.

### Part VI — Installation (Ch 32–33) · *this is the sales engine*
32. **The 30-Day Rollout** — week by week. What a manager runs on which day,
    what gets recorded, what gets scored, what to do when adoption stalls.
33. **The Manager's Scorecard** — the full audit instrument. Every stage,
    every metric, the rubric, and the coaching conversation that follows a
    bad score.

Chapters 32 and 33 do more for the consulting rate than the other 31
combined. They hand a manager a complete implementation plan and, in doing
so, show them exactly how much easier it is to have the author run it. That
is not a bait-and-switch — the plan genuinely works unassisted. It just makes
the value of the install legible and specific instead of hypothetical.

## 5. Editing rules (mechanical)

- **Second person, present tense, imperative mood.** Default voice.
- **First person is rationed.** Reserved for evidence blocks. If "I" opens a
  chapter, the chapter gets rewritten.
- **No chapter opens with a story.** Every chapter opens with the standard.
- **Every claim gets a mechanism.** Not "this works" but "this works because
  a named emotion stops being a weapon."
- **Kill every unfalsifiable sentence.** If it can't be wrong, it isn't a
  standard, and a corporate reader will smell it.
- **Scripts are typographically separate.** Boxed, monospaced, quotable.
  A reader flipping the book should be able to find the words in two seconds.
- **Reading level: operational.** Written for a tired rep at 7am, not for a
  reviewer. Short sentences carry the instructions.
- **Length target:** 1,800–2,600 words per chapter. Long enough to be a
  standard, short enough to be used. ~70K words total.
- **Attribution stays honest.** The FBI-derived tools are cited as
  FBI-derived. The proprietary contribution is the sequencing, the drills,
  and the scoring — that claim is defensible and stronger than a false one.

## 6. Front and back matter (the funnel plumbing)

Front matter is what the Amazon "Look Inside" sample shows. It is the highest
traffic real estate in the entire business and should be written last, most
carefully.

- **Title page positioning line** — "A field manual," not "a guide," not "a
  journey."
- **Who this manual is for** — one page, naming job titles explicitly. Sales
  leaders, procurement, claims, legal, real estate principals, L&D. A reader
  who finds their own title on page one has already decided.
- **The credentials page** — Marine, 1,000+ live sessions since 2024, daily
  live coaching. Compressed, factual, no narrative.
- **Back matter** — the 33 Field Cards as an appendix; the emotion
  vocabulary reference; the scorecard blank; and a single clean page on
  bringing the install in-house that names the ladder without pitching.

## 7. Process

Chapter by chapter. For each one:

1. Read the source chapter from the PDF.
2. Extract: which tool(s), which stories, which claims survive.
3. Rewrite to the seven-part spine.
4. Write the Field Card.
5. Flag anything that needs Dan's real field material — actual client
   situations, actual numbers, actual objections. Those get marked
   `[NEEDS: ...]` rather than invented.

Nothing in this book gets fabricated. Where the manual needs a real case, a
real metric, or a real client outcome, it gets flagged for Dan to fill.
