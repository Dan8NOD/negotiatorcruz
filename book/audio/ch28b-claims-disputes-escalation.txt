Part 1 of 2 covers the standard, when to reach for it, the words, and what good sounds like.

The drill

"Hold the Line at Volume." Twenty minutes, pairs, recorded. The hardest
drill in the manual.
The counterpart plays an escalated party with a real grievance - from an
 actual matter, not invented.
They open loud, and they stay loud for the first two minutes.
The operator may use only Read and Disarm tools. No solutions, no policy,
 no numbers. Four minutes minimum before Steer is permitted.
Score two things: did the operator's volume rise, and how long before they
 offered something.
Only after the temperature visibly drops may they move to Steer.

Most people offer a solution inside ninety seconds. Watching yourself do it on
tape - and watching the counterpart's temperature rise in response - is the
lesson.

Scored on

Metric: Time in Read. Target: Four minutes minimum before any solution is offered. Timed.

Metric: Story completed. Target: The counterpart finished their account without interruption. Binary.

Metric: Volume differential. Target: Operator's volume stayed flat while theirs rose. Scored from tape.

Metric: Small thing delivered. Target: Whatever they named, done same-day. Binary.

Metric: Grievance summarized. Target: A "that's right" obtained on the grievance, not just the facts.

Metric: Policy position. Target: "Our terms state" never appears in the first five minutes. Binary.

Failure mode

Solving before the story is finished. The handler hears enough to identify
the category, recognizes the fix, and offers it - at ninety seconds, while the
other person is still explaining.

The offer is often reasonable and it will be refused, because accepting it
would concede that the explanation was unnecessary. Now the settlement has to
be larger than it needed to be, purely to compensate for the interruption.

On tape: a number, a date, or the word "policy" inside the first two
minutes.

The recovery:

Quote. I jumped in before you'd finished and that wasn't fair. Start again from where the problem actually began - I won't interrupt. End quote.

Then don't.

The second failure mode: defending the organization. The handler, feeling
the accusation land on their employer, begins explaining why the process worked
as designed. Chapter 14 - every sentence of defence extends the dispute,
because the counterpart's actual position is that something went wrong, and
arguing that nothing went wrong makes settlement the only remaining way for
them to be right.
