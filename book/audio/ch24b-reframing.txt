Part IV · CLOSE

Part 1 of 2 covers the standard, when to reach for it, the words, and what good sounds like.

The drill

"Two Frames." Twenty minutes, pairs.
The counterpart states a stuck objection from a real deal - something that's
 been argued twice with no movement.
The operator writes two descriptions of the same deal: one framed as
 today's cost, one framed as twelve months out. Same facts, both true.
The operator delivers the second as a vision label and holds silence.
Then a second pass: the operator adds one elevation label, and the room
 scores only one thing - is it true? Anything the operator wouldn't say
 about this person to a colleague is struck.
Swap.

Step 2 is the drill. Writing both frames out proves that reframing isn't
inventing - the facts don't change between the two descriptions, only which
ones are load-bearing. Reps who see that stop worrying that reframing is
dishonest, which is the objection that otherwise keeps them from using it.

Scored on

Metric: Frame named. Target: Rep can state, out loud, what frame the negotiation is currently in. If they can't, they're inside it rather than working it.

Metric: Vision before terms re-open. Target: A vision label deployed before returning to numbers on any stuck deal. Binary.

Metric: Elevation truth test. Target: Would the rep say it to a colleague about this person? Anything failing scores zero.

Metric: Density. Target: One reframe at a time. Two in a row scores as a failure.

Metric: Future stated in their words. Target: The counterpart's description of the working outcome, documented. This becomes the delivery specification.

Failure mode

Reframing instead of solving. There is a real problem - an implementation
that went badly, a price genuinely above budget, a missing capability - and the
rep, reaching for a tool, reframes it. "I think the bigger picture here is
where you want to be in a year."

That is spin, it is heard as spin, and it costs more credibility than the
original problem did. A reframe changes which true things are in the
foreground. It cannot make an unsolved problem stop being one.

What it looks like on tape: a vision label immediately following a concrete
objection, with nothing addressing the objection in between.

The recovery: go back and take the problem seriously.

Quote. I skipped past that and I shouldn't have. The implementation issue is real. Let's deal with it before I talk about anything else. End quote.

The second failure mode: elevation that's transparently instrumental. Three
elevation labels in a meeting, all arriving just before an ask, is a pattern
the counterpart will spot and will resent - because the implication is that
they're the kind of person who can be moved by being told they're impressive.
One per conversation, unattached to a request, delivered as an observation and
then dropped.
