The drill

"Coach the Coach." Thirty minutes, managers only, run monthly.
Managers pair up. One brings a real rep recording and coaches the other
 manager as though they were the rep.
The observer scores four things only:
Did they ask for the rep's read first?
Did they name one behaviour, not several?
Did they make the rep rehearse in the room?
Did they agree one measurable commitment?
Swap.
Then the reversal: the observing manager plays a rep who politely resists.
 Score whether the coach named the resistance or pushed through it.

Managers are coached far less than reps are, and it shows. This drill is the
one that makes internal certification (Chapter 2's Level ladder) credible - a
manager who can't pass it can't sign anyone off.

Scored on

Metric: Coaching cadence. Target: One 20-minute session per rep per week. Binary, tracked.

Metric: One-thing discipline. Target: Exactly one behaviour per session. Two or more scores as a failure.

Metric: Rehearsal occurred. Target: The rep performed the behaviour in the room. Binary - this is the row that predicts change.

Metric: Rep-first diagnosis. Target: The manager asked for the rep's read before giving theirs. Binary.

Metric: Commitment measurable. Target: Named behaviour, named check, named day.

Metric: Follow-through. Target: The manager actually checked on the agreed day. Track it - this is where programmes die.

The last row is the quiet one. Rollouts rarely fail because the coaching was
wrong; they fail because week three got busy and nobody checked, and the floor
learned that the commitment was optional.

Failure mode

Coaching by demonstration. The manager listens, identifies the gap, and
fills it with their own competence. "What I'd have said there is…" The rep
nods. Nothing changes, and both parties leave believing coaching happened.

What it looks like: the manager talking more than the rep in a coaching
session. Score talk time in coaching the same way you score it in negotiation - 
under 40%, same target, same reason.

The recovery: stop and hand it back.

Quote. I've been telling you what I'd do, which isn't useful. Run it again - I'll be the buyer, you find your own words. End quote.

The second failure mode: coaching everything. The manager, seeing four
correctable behaviours, corrects four. The rep implements none, because
attention is finite and four simultaneous changes to how you speak under
pressure is not a thing a human does. Pick the one that costs the most money - 
usually Disarm being absent, or concessions after silence - and coach only that
until it holds. The others will still be there in three weeks, and several will
have fixed themselves.
