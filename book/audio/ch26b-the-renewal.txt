Chapter 26 - The Renewal and the Price Increase

Part 2 of 2 · The Practice

Part V · APPLICATIONS

Part 1 of 2 covers the standard, when to reach for it, the words, and what good sounds like.

The drill

"Write Their Review." Thirty minutes, run ninety days before any renewal.
The rep writes the internal review the client would write about you - 
 in the client's voice, including the criticism.
They mark each line known (they've said it), suspected, or
 invented.
Anything in the suspected column becomes a question for the next call,
 written verbatim.
Then: list everyone on the client side who would be consulted, and mark who
 has spoken to you in the last ninety days. The gap is the risk.
Finally, name which of the three leverages you actually hold. If the answer
 is only positive, the renewal is weaker than the forecast says.

Scored on

Metric: Read window. Target: Renewal work begins 90+ days out. Binary.

Metric: Criticism surfaced. Target: At least one genuine complaint obtained before terms are discussed.

Metric: Audit precedes increase. Target: The increase is never the first thing said. Binary.

Metric: Leverage named. Target: Rep can state which of the three types they hold. Positive-only is a flag.

Metric: Committee mapped. Target: Renewal stakeholders identified and contacted, not assumed.

Failure mode

Treating the renewal as an administrative event. The rep sends the paper,
assumes continuity, and discovers in week two that the account has been
quietly unhappy for eight months and has already taken a competitor call.

Nobody escalates a slow decline. They just don't renew.

On tape: the phrase "just a formality" anywhere in a renewal conversation.

The recovery:

Quote. I've been treating this like paperwork and I don't think I've earned that. What's the honest version of how this year went for you? End quote.

The second failure mode: apologising for the increase. The rep raises price
and then spends four minutes justifying it, discounting the number's legitimacy
with every additional sentence. Audit the objection, state the mechanism once,
and hold the silence (Chapter 8). A price you defend at length is a price you
have already signalled is negotiable.
