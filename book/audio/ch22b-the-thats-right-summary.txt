Chapter 22: The "That's Right" Summary

Part 2 of 2 · The Practice

Part IV · CLOSE

Part 1 of 2 covers the standard, when to reach for it, the words, and what good sounds like.

The drill

"Hunt the Phrase." Twenty minutes, pairs, recorded.
The counterpart describes a real negotiation they're in, for three minutes.
The operator may use encouragers, one focusing label, and then one
 summary, and the summary must aim at the emotional truth rather than the
 terms.
The counterpart says only one of three things: "that's right," "you're
 right," or "no."
If it isn't "that's right," the operator gets two more attempts and
 nothing else. No new questions.
Swap. Track attempts-to-land across the room over a month.

Rule four is the drill. Restricting the operator to re-summarizing forces them
to work with what they already heard rather than gathering more, and that is
the actual skill. Most reps have the material and are naming the wrong layer
of it.

Scored on

Metric: "That's right" obtained. Target: Before any closing ask. Binary, per deal. The single most predictive gate in the method.

Metric: Attempts to land. Target: Under two. Three or more means the rep is summarizing logistics.

Metric: Emotional content. Target: Does the summary name something unstated, or only recap terms? Scored from tape.

Metric: "You're right" logged separately. Target: Never counted as a pass. Track the ratio. A rep with lots of "you're right" is being managed.

Metric: Clean ending. Target: Nothing follows the summary. No ask attached. Binary.

Failure mode

Accepting "you're right." The rep summarizes, hears "you're right," marks
it as agreement, and closes. Two weeks later the deal dies for a reason nobody
mentioned during the call.

You're right is what people say when they want the conversation to end. It is
agreement with your sentence, not with your understanding, and the
distinction is the difference between a deal and a delay.

What it looks like on tape: "you're right," followed by the rep's closing
question inside ten seconds.

The recovery: notice it out loud and go again.

Quote. That felt like a polite yes rather than a real one, and I'd rather get it right. What am I still missing? End quote.

The second failure mode: summarizing the terms. The rep produces a clean,
accurate recap of price, timeline, and scope and waits for "that's right." It
doesn't come, because nothing in the summary required them to feel understood. It required them to check arithmetic. Terms are what Chapter 17's working
summary is for. This chapter is for the sentence underneath the terms, and if
you can deliver that one accurately, the terms tend to stop being the argument.
