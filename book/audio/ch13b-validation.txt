The drill

"Validate, Don't Concede." Twenty minutes, pairs, and it is harder than it
sounds.
The counterpart states an objection the operator genuinely disagrees with.
 Real ones from real pipeline.
The operator must validate it, empathy statement or positive label, and
 concede nothing. No movement on terms, no "we could look at," no
 softening of the number.
A third person listens for one thing only: the word "but." Any "but,"
 "however," or "that said" immediately following the validation ends the
 round.
Then a second pass where the operator adds one trust-repair question and
 stops talking.
Swap.

The "but" rule is the drill. Almost everyone validates correctly and then
detonates it within four words, because the validation feels like a debt that
has to be immediately balanced. It isn't. You can validate and then simply be
quiet, and the quiet is where the work happens.

Scored on

Metric: Validation without concession. Target: Position validated, terms unmoved. Binary, and the core skill.

Metric: "But" count. Target: Zero within two sentences of a validation.

Metric: Truth test. Target: Would the operator say the positive label about this person to a colleague? If not, it was flattery and scores zero.

Metric: Trust question asked. Target: At least one trust-repair question in any deal where prior disappointment surfaced. Binary.

Metric: Defending stopped. Target: Did the counterpart stop restating their position after the validation? Scored from tape.

Failure mode

Validation as a preamble to the rebuttal. The rep says the right sentence,
then attaches the argument they were always going to make. "That completely
makes sense, but I do want to point out that our SLA is actually…"

The counterpart hears one thing: a technique deployed to soften them up before
the real message. And now the validation tool is dead for the rest of the
relationship, because they've learned what it precedes.

What it looks like on tape: the validation and the rebuttal in the same
breath. No pause between them.

The recovery: name it and separate them.

Quote. I did that badly. I validated you and then immediately argued with you. Let me just leave the first part where it is. That does make sense. End quote.

Then stop. The repair works because it demonstrates that the validation was
real enough to be worth rescuing.

The second failure mode: validating so thoroughly you agree. The
over-empathetic rep validates the objection, validates the emotion, validates
the history, and by minute six has verbally endorsed the buyer's position so
completely that asking for anything would be inconsistent. Validation is a
bridge, not a destination. If you've validated for more than about ninety
seconds without moving toward Stage 3, you aren't disarming, you're agreeing
slowly.
