The drill

"Where's It From." Twenty minutes, pairs.
The counterpart states a number and a one-line justification. Real ones from
 real deals.
The operator may only use anchor probes. Four minutes, no counter-offers, no
 defending their own price.
Goal: get the counterpart to say what the number is genuinely built on, or
 to admit it isn't built on anything.
Then the bracket round: the operator must obtain a range without naming a
 figure.
Swap. Compare how many numbers survived the probe.

Most rooms discover that a third to a half of the numbers they've been treating
as constraints dissolve under two questions. That discovery does more for a
floor's confidence than any amount of anchoring theory.

Scored on

Metric: Probe before counter. Target: Every counterpart number probed before any counter-offer. Binary.

Metric: Numbers that dissolved. Target: Count of figures that changed or lost their justification under probing. Track monthly.

Metric: Range obtained. Target: A range in the counterpart's words before either side names a point. Binary.

Metric: Ladder discipline. Target: Concessions decelerate. Any equal or growing step scores as a failure.

Metric: Non-round final. Target: Final number is precise, not round. Binary.

Metric: Trades between steps. Target: Each ladder step paired with an ask. Zero unreciprocated steps.

Failure mode

Countering a number you never tested. They say sixty. The rep, trained to
never accept the first offer, counters at seventy-five, and in doing so has
accepted that sixty is a real number and that this negotiation is about the gap
between two figures.

It might not have been a real number. Two questions would have established
that. Instead the rep has legitimized the anchor and converted a conversation
about value into arithmetic, which is the terrain where the buyer with the
tighter constraint always wins.

What it looks like on tape: a counter-offer inside sixty seconds of their
number, with no question in between.

The recovery: you can still probe after countering, but you must own the
sequence.

Quote. I jumped to a counter without asking the obvious thing. Where did the sixty come from? End quote.

The second failure mode: even concessions. The rep moves 10, then 10, then
From the buyer's chair that is not a floor, it is a rate, and the correct
response to a rate is to keep asking. Every equal step you take is a promise
that another one exists.
