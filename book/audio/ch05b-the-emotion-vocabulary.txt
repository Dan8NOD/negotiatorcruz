Part 1 of 2 covers the standard, when to reach for it, the words, and what good sounds like.

The drill

"Three Emotions, Three Minutes." Fifteen minutes, weekly, no prep. The
foundation drill of Part I.
Play three minutes of a real recorded call.
Everyone stays silent and writes three emotions they believe the
 counterpart is in.
Next to each, the evidence, the exact phrase, the pause, the shift in
 pace. No evidence, no credit.
Everyone also writes the state: Torn, Ready, or Not Yet.
Around the table. Two rules for scoring: "frustrated" scores zero, it is
 the "loud" of emotional vocabulary, and any emotion without an evidence
 line scores zero regardless of how right it sounds.
Compare the state calls. Where the room splits, replay that thirty seconds
 and argue about it. The argument is the training.

The variant that teaches the most: run it once with the audio only, then
again with the transcript only. The gap between what the room hears and what
the room reads is where somatic reading lives, and nobody believes how large
that gap is until they've watched it happen to them.

Scored on

Metric: Specificity. Target: Named state is specific, cornered, exposed, worn down, outflanked, not generic. The four default words score zero.

Metric: Evidence attached. Target: Every named emotion has a citable moment. Under 100% means guessing.

Metric: State accuracy. Target: Torn / Ready / Not Yet called correctly, verified against how the deal actually moved. Track over a month.

Metric: Somatic restraint. Target: No more than one somatic read per ten minutes. Over-use is scored as a failure, not as enthusiasm.

Metric: Ready detection. Target: Of deals that closed, how many were identified as Ready before the last call. This is the number with money attached.

That last row deserves attention from anyone running a floor. Reps who
routinely miss the Ready state are re-selling closed deals, and the cost shows
up as cycle length rather than lost revenue, which is why nobody catches it.

Failure mode

Reaching for the same three adjectives. The vocabulary collapses back to
default under pressure, which is exactly when accuracy matters most. You will
watch a rep who named "outmaneuvered by their own procurement team" on
Tuesday say "seems like you're frustrated" on Thursday to a buyer who is
plainly humiliated. Same rep. Higher stakes. Worse instrument.

What it looks like on tape: the same emotion word appearing in three
different calls with three different counterparts.

The recovery: ask instead of assert. When the precise word won't come,
don't reach for a bad one.

Quote. Something about this is landing harder than the rest of it, and I don't want to guess at what. What is it? End quote.

Admitting you can't name it reads as honesty rather than incompetence, and it
gets you the word from the only person who actually has it.

The second failure mode: diagnosing the person instead of the moment.
Deciding in minute three that this is An Anxious Buyer and then filtering
everything after through that. States are weather, not climate. The same person
is Not Yet at 9am and Torn by 9:40 because something you said moved them, which is the entire point of the work, and you'll miss it if you've already
filed them.
