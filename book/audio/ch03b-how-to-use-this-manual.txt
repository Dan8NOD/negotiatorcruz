The first week

Before Chapter 4, run this. It takes no preparation and it establishes the
baseline everything else is measured against.
Record three of your own conversations. Real ones. With permission
 where required, and note that asking for permission is itself a Stage 1
 move worth observing.
Listen to them once, without judgment. Just once.
Listen again with a timer. Mark your talk-time percentage.
Mark, on each one, the stage you were actually in every thirty seconds. The drill from Chapter 2.
Write down three things you already do well. This is not
 encouragement. Most operators have one or two stages they run competently
 and don't know it, and building on those is faster than repairing the
 others.

Keep those three recordings. In ninety days you will want a baseline, and you
will not be able to reconstruct one from memory, memory edits negotiations in
your favor with remarkable consistency.

Scored on

For an individual operator, week one only:

Metric: Baseline captured. Target: Three recordings, stage-mapped, talk-time marked. Binary.

Metric: Path identified. Target: You know which of the three readers you are and where you're starting. Binary.

Metric: Honest talk time. Target: You have a real number, not an estimate. Most people guess 50% and measure 70%.

Failure mode

Reading it. Just reading it.

The manual is engineered to be satisfying to read, which is a liability. You
will finish a chapter, recognize a mistake you have made a hundred times, feel
the click of understanding, and mistake that feeling for a change in
capability. It isn't one. It is the same feeling available from any well-made
book, and it decays on roughly the same schedule as a motivational seminar, about seventy-two hours.

What it looks like: you can explain labeling to a colleague, and you have
not delivered one.

The recovery: put the book down mid-chapter and go run the drill badly.
Badly is the operative word. The first attempt at a label out loud, to a real
person, with your own voice doing something unfamiliar, teaches more than the
chapter it came from. That gap, between understanding this and being able to
do it under pressure, is the only thing this manual is actually about.
