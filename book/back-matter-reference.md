# Appendix B: Reference

Three things a manager needs at hand and should not have to hunt a chapter
for: the vocabulary, the blank instrument, and the honest page about what
happens if you want help installing this.

## B1. The emotion vocabulary

Chapter 5 argues that most negotiators run on four words. This is the list to
reach for instead.

**How to use it.** Do not memorize it. Read it once a week for a month until
the words are available under pressure, which is the only condition that
matters. Then use the counterpart's word, never yours, the moment they give
you one.

### Pressure and constraint

cornered · boxed in · squeezed · pinned · stuck · hemmed in · out of road ·
overextended · stretched · underwater · behind · exposed

### Status and standing

undermined · overlooked · talked past · dismissed · sidelined · outranked ·
second-guessed · not taken seriously · embarrassed · shown up

### Trust and history

burned · let down · blindsided · strung along · sold something else last time ·
wary · guarded · once bitten · unconvinced · waiting for the catch

### Control and authority

powerless · overruled · handed someone else's answer · out of the loop ·
last to know · a messenger · asking permission · fenced in

### Effort and fatigue

worn down · ground down · tired of explaining · repeating yourself · carrying
it alone · uncredited · out of patience · done

### Wanting it to work

invested · hopeful and hedging · protective of it · rooting for it · willing
but constrained · closer than you sound

**The four to strike.** *Frustrated, concerned, upset, excited.* All four are
the emotional equivalent of describing an engine as "loud." They score zero on
the Chapter 5 rubric and they are what everyone reaches for first.

### The state triage on top of the vocabulary

Specific words tell you what someone feels. The state tells you what to do
about it, and the state is the more actionable read.

| | **TORN** | **READY** | **NOT YET** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sounds like** | repeats both sides unprompted, asks for information they don't use | asks about start dates, corrects your caution | warm, agreeable, reschedules easily |
| **Time =** | safety | waste | abundant |
| **Silence =** | doubt building | something's wrong | relief |
| **Do** | name the tension, remove the shame | stop selling, start scheduling | find the future where the problem exists |
| **Never** | add options or pressure | re-argue the settled case | manufacture urgency |

## B2. The scorecard, blank

Twelve rows. Score the call, not the person. One row of feedback per session,
never twelve.

```
REP ______________________  CALL ____________  DATE __________
SCORED BY ________________  TAPE LENGTH __________

READ
  1  Talk time under 40%                              [  ]
  2  A label before any number                        [  ]
  3  Label, then silence held                         [  ]
  4  Correction taken cleanly, no defending           [  ]
  5  Decision structure surfaced (a name, a process)  [  ]

DISARM
  6  Objection named before they raised it            [  ]
  7  No undefended defending                          [  ]

STEER
  8  Calibrated question before any number            [  ]
  9  No unrequested concession                        [  ]
 10  Summary drew agreement                           [  ]

CLOSE
 11  Silence held five seconds or more after a number [  ]
 12  Next action, owner and date, all three           [  ]

                                            TOTAL ____ / 12

 0-4    Start at row 1. Nothing else until talk time moves.
 5-7    Typical untrained floor. Rows 6 and 7 are usually absent.
 8-10   Strong. Coach the two rows they never score on.
11-12   Give them a harder call, not more coaching.

MONEY ROWS: 9 and 11. If you track two rows across a floor, track these.

ONE ROW ONLY: "Next two calls, only row ____."

TIMESTAMP ______  BEHAVIOR ________________________________
QUESTION ________________________________________________
```

**Reproduction.** Copy this page freely for internal use on your own floor.
That permission is deliberate: a scorecard nobody can photocopy is a scorecard
nobody uses.

## B3. Bringing it in-house

The honest page, and the last one in the book.

Everything in this manual works unassisted. Chapters 31 through 33 are a
complete installation: the four-week rollout, the coaching cadence, and the
audit instrument. A capable manager can run all of it without ever speaking to
me, and a fair number have.

What you will not get from the book is the part that depends on somebody
watching your people negotiate. Reading a drill is not the same as having a
room corrected while it runs one, and the gap between those two is the whole
reason the practice sessions exist.

So, plainly, and once:

**Free.** Daily live coaching, and a free practice app with the full tool
catalog. Run it for a month before you spend anything. If your team will not
open a free app, a paid training day will not fix that, and you will have
learned something worth knowing for nothing.

**The written method.** This manual, plus the guide that covers the deal arc
around the conversation. Buy these before anything else. If the writing does
not convince you, the training day will not either.

**One hour, private, on your actual deal.** Useful when there is a specific
negotiation in front of you this week and getting it right is worth more than
the hour costs.

**A day with your floor.** Six hours, your office, your real pipeline as the
roleplay material, every round recorded. Everyone leaves with the field cards
and a scorecard showing exactly where they are weak.

**A multi-day install with follow-up.** Where the habit actually sets, because
the thirty-day follow-up is included rather than sold. This is the rung that
does what Chapter 31 describes, with someone in the room.

**Certification.** Your managers licensed to run the Protocol on their own
floor: to score, to run the drills, to sign people off. This is the version
where I eventually stop being necessary, which is the correct end state for
any methodology worth adopting.

Current rates and the booking link live at **negotiatorcruz.com**. They are
published rather than quoted, because a price you have to ask for is a price
that changes depending on who is asking.
