# Chapter 29 — Negotiating Internally: Up, Down, and Sideways

**Part V · APPLICATIONS**

*Part 1 of 2 covers the standard, when to reach for it, the words, and what good sounds like.*

---

## The drill

**"No Exit."** Twenty minutes, pairs, using real internal situations.

1. Each person brings a live internal negotiation — a request they need to make
   or a demand they're absorbing.
2. Run it with one hard constraint: **the operator may not escalate, may not
   invoke authority, and may not threaten a deadline.**
3. Second constraint: **the operator has to work with this person again next
   week**, and the counterpart is instructed to remember how it went.
4. Score the outcome and the relationship separately. Both, every time.
5. Then the reversal: run the same scenario with escalation permitted, and
   compare what the counterpart says afterwards about how they'd behave in the
   next negotiation.

Step 5 makes the argument better than any explanation. The escalated version
usually wins the scenario and visibly damages the next one.

---

## Scored on

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| **Relationship scored separately** | Every internal negotiation scored twice — outcome and relationship. Both recorded. |
| **Escalations used** | As close to zero as the work allows. Track it; it's a leading indicator of a manager who's running out of influence. |
| **Trades made** | Peer requests paid for with something. Unreciprocated internal asks tracked the same way as external concessions. |
| **Disagreement surfaced** | Reports state objections before execution rather than during. Binary per decision. |
| **Audit used upward** | Objections named before the senior person raises them. Binary. |

---

## Failure mode

**Winning the meeting.** The rep or manager builds a case, presents it well,
and prevails in the room — in front of an audience, against a colleague who now
has to explain the loss to their own team.

The decision reverses within two weeks, quietly, through channels you don't
see. Nobody tells you why. And the next time you need that function's
cooperation, it will be technically provided and practically useless.

**What it looks like:** a decision that goes your way in the meeting and
doesn't happen.

**The recovery:** go find them, privately, and give something back.

```
  "That went my way in the room and I don't think it
   landed well. What did I miss about your side of it?"
```

**The second failure mode: treating internal negotiation as beneath the
method.** People who run the Protocol carefully with a $200K prospect will walk
into a resourcing conversation with a peer and open with a demand — because it's
"just internal." Internal negotiations are more frequent, more consequential to
a career, and harder, because you cannot leave. They deserve the same sequence.
