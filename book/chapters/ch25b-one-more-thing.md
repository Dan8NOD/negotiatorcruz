## The drill

**"The Sweep."** Fifteen minutes, pairs, and it is deliberately uncomfortable.

1. Operator and counterpart run to a verbal agreement on a real deal, or pick
   up from a real one that's about to close.
2. The operator delivers a sweep question and **holds six seconds.**
3. The counterpart's instruction: name one thing you were genuinely holding
   back. Not invented. A real one from the real deal.
4. The operator must respond in one of exactly two ways: **grant it, or trade
   for it** (Chapter 18). Deflecting, deferring, or explaining why it's
   difficult scores zero.
5. Swap. Then, around the room: how many of those held-back items would have
   surfaced later anyway?

Step 5 is where the argument gets made. Most rooms find that the majority of
held-back items would have appeared eventually, as friction, as a support
escalation, as a harder renewal, and the difference is that later they arrive
with no goodwill attached.

---

## Scored on

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| **Sweep delivered** | Between verbal agreement and execution, every deal. Binary. On untrained floors this is near zero. |
| **Held-back items surfaced** | Count per deal. Zero across many deals means the question is being delivered as a formality. |
| **Response quality** | Granted or traded. Deflections scored as failures. |
| **Cost of granted items** | Track it. The number is almost always trivially small, and that's the case for the practice. |
| **Early implementation friction** | Issues raised in the first thirty days. This is the number the sweep is meant to reduce. |

---

## Failure mode

**Asking as a formality.** The rep delivers the sweep in the tone of a flight
attendant, *"anything else for you today?"*, with the paperwork already
sliding across. The counterpart correctly reads it as a closing ritual rather
than a real question and says no.

Nothing was gathered. Worse, the rep now believes there was nothing to gather,
and will be surprised in week three.

**What it looks like on tape:** the sweep question and the next sentence
separated by under two seconds, or the sweep delivered while discussing
logistics.

**The recovery:** stop, and ask it properly.

```
  "I asked that like a formality and I actually mean it.
   What's the thing you almost brought up and didn't?"
```

**The second failure mode: asking and then flinching.** They name something
small, and the rep, surprised, at the finish line, unwilling to reopen
anything, hedges. *"Ah, that might be tricky, let me check with the team."*

You invited the request and then punished them for making it. The cost of that
is not the item. It is that you have taught them, at the very start of the
relationship, that asking you for things is unpleasant. That lesson gets
applied for the length of the contract. Decide before you ask what you can give
away, and give it away cleanly.
