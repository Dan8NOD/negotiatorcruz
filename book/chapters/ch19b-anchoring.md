# Chapter 19 — Anchoring

### Part 2 of 2 · The Practice

**Part III · STEER**

*Part 1 of 2 covers the standard, when to reach for it, the words, and what good sounds like.*

---

## The drill

**"Where's It From."** Twenty minutes, pairs.

1. The counterpart states a number and a one-line justification. Real ones from
   real deals.
2. The operator may only use anchor probes. Four minutes, no counter-offers, no
   defending their own price.
3. Goal: get the counterpart to say what the number is genuinely built on — or
   to admit it isn't built on anything.
4. Then the bracket round: the operator must obtain a range without naming a
   figure.
5. Swap. Compare how many numbers survived the probe.

Most rooms discover that a third to a half of the numbers they've been treating
as constraints dissolve under two questions. That discovery does more for a
floor's confidence than any amount of anchoring theory.

---

## Scored on

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| **Probe before counter** | Every counterpart number probed before any counter-offer. Binary. |
| **Numbers that dissolved** | Count of figures that changed or lost their justification under probing. Track monthly. |
| **Range obtained** | A range in the counterpart's words before either side names a point. Binary. |
| **Ladder discipline** | Concessions decelerate. Any equal or growing step scores as a failure. |
| **Non-round final** | Final number is precise, not round. Binary. |
| **Trades between steps** | Each ladder step paired with an ask. Zero unreciprocated steps. |

---

## Failure mode

**Countering a number you never tested.** They say sixty. The rep, trained to
never accept the first offer, counters at seventy-five — and in doing so has
accepted that sixty is a real number and that this negotiation is about the gap
between two figures.

It might not have been a real number. Two questions would have established
that. Instead the rep has legitimized the anchor and converted a conversation
about value into arithmetic, which is the terrain where the buyer with the
tighter constraint always wins.

**What it looks like on tape:** a counter-offer inside sixty seconds of their
number, with no question in between.

**The recovery:** you can still probe after countering, but you must own the
sequence.

```
  "I jumped to a counter without asking the obvious thing.
   Where did the sixty come from?"
```

**The second failure mode: even concessions.** The rep moves 10, then 10, then
10. From the buyer's chair that is not a floor, it is a rate — and the correct
response to a rate is to keep asking. Every equal step you take is a promise
that another one exists.
