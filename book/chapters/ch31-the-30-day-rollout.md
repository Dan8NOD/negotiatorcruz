# Chapter 31 — The 30-Day Rollout

**Part VI · Installation**

---

## The Standard

A rollout is the four-week sequence that converts a team who has read this
manual into a team who runs it under pressure. It is not a training event. An
event is something that happens to your people; a rollout is something your
managers do, on a schedule, with recordings and a score.

The distinction matters because the failure mode of negotiation training is
well documented and it is not "the material was wrong." It is decay. A team
comes out of a strong session at high confidence, runs the new behavior for
roughly seventy-two hours, hits one live call where it doesn't work cleanly,
and reverts. By day twenty the floor sounds exactly like it did before,
everyone remembers the training fondly, and nothing on the pipeline report
moved.

This chapter exists to defeat day twenty. Everything in it is built around one
principle: **a behavior that is not recorded and scored within a week of being
taught will not survive the month.** Not because your people are lazy — because
under quota pressure, humans revert to whatever is automatic, and four weeks is
roughly what it takes to make a new sentence automatic.

You run this yourself. It requires no outside facilitator, and it works
unassisted. That is a deliberate design choice, and an honest one: if you run
these four weeks properly you will get most of the available result. What you
will not get is the part that depends on someone watching your reps negotiate
and telling them the specific thing they personally are doing wrong — and that
gap is the real argument for an install, not anything withheld from this page.

---

## When to reach for it

Run the full four weeks when:

- **A team has just been through the manual, a training day, or both.** The
  rollout is the retention layer. Without it the day is entertainment.
- **You've inherited a floor with no shared negotiation language.** Four weeks
  gives you a vocabulary your coaching can reference.
- **Win rates are fine but margins are eroding.** This is the specific signature
  of a team that concedes to close. Weeks 2 and 4 target it directly.

Do **not** run it when:

- **You can't protect fifteen minutes on Monday for four consecutive weeks.** A
  rollout that skips week 3 is worse than no rollout — it teaches your floor
  that initiatives here are optional, which raises the cost of the next one.
- **You aren't willing to record.** Every mechanism in this chapter depends on
  reviewable tape. Coaching from memory reliably produces coaching about
  personality rather than behavior.
- **Your team's problem is qualification, not negotiation.** Reps losing to "no
  decision" are not losing on negotiation technique. Fix the pipeline first;
  this rollout will not help and will cost you a month of credibility.
- **Fewer than four people.** Below that, run individual coaching rounds. The
  drills in this chapter depend on pairs and an observer.

## The words

The rollout has its own scripts — not negotiation lines, but the four sentences
a manager says to launch it. These matter more than they look. The most common
way a rollout dies is a manager introducing it apologetically.

```
THE LAUNCH — Monday of week 1, before any drill

  "For the next four weeks we're running one tool at a time.
   Fifteen minutes Monday, recorded calls Wednesday and
   Thursday, fifteen minutes Friday to score them. That's
   the whole commitment. I'm not adding anything else to
   your week and I'm not taking the four weeks back."
```

```
THE RECORDING FRAME — say this before anyone objects to it

  "We're recording so I can coach the sentence, not the
   person. If I coach from memory I end up telling you that
   you sounded unsure, which is useless. With tape I can tell
   you that you labeled and then kept talking for nine
   seconds. That, you can fix."
```

```
THE STALL — week 3, when adoption visibly drops

  "It stopped working this week for most of you. That's the
   part of the curve where people quit, so we're not going
   to. Bring me the call where it went worst and we'll run
   that one."
```

```
THE HANDOFF — Friday of week 4

  "From Monday this stops being a program and becomes how we
   review calls. Same scorecard, same fifteen minutes, no
   end date."
```

That last one is the entire point of the rollout. A program that ends leaves
nothing behind. A program that converts into your standing call-review format
has permanently changed how the floor works.

## What good sounds like

You are watching for three signals over the four weeks, in this order.

**Week 1–2 — awkwardness.** Your reps will sound stilted, and several will tell
you the labels feel fake. This is correct and you should say so out loud. A new
sentence delivered smoothly in week 1 usually means the rep isn't actually
changing anything — they've mapped the new tool onto what they already did.
Visible awkwardness is evidence of genuine behavior change.

**Week 3 — the dip.** Adoption drops, complaints rise, and at least one senior
rep will explain that this doesn't work in your particular market. Expect it on
schedule. It is not feedback about the method; it is what the end of the novelty
period feels like from the inside. Managers who treat the week-3 dip as a signal
to stop are the single largest cause of failed rollouts.

**Week 4 — unprompted use.** The signal you actually want: a rep uses a tool in
a call you weren't reviewing, and mentions it in passing as though it were
obvious. That is the behavior going automatic. When you hear it, name it in
front of the floor.

What you are *not* listening for is enthusiasm. Enthusiasm peaks in week 1 and
predicts nothing. Talk-time ratios in week 4 predict everything.

## The drill

Fifteen minutes, Monday, same room, same time. The structure never changes —
only the tool does.

```
THE MONDAY FIFTEEN

  0–2 min    Name the tool. Read the standard aloud. No discussion.
  2–5 min    You demonstrate it badly on purpose, then correctly.
             The bad version first is not a joke — it gives the
             room permission to be bad at it in the next ten minutes.
  5–12 min   Pairs. One negotiator, one counterpart, one observer.
             Three rounds, ~2 min each, rotate roles each round.
             Observer's only job: count. Not advise — count.
  12–15 min  Observers report numbers only. No opinions.
             "He labeled twice and both times kept talking."
```

**The four weeks:**

| Week | Stage | Tool focus | Recorded metric |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | READ | Labels, silence, minimal encouragers | Talk-time ratio |
| 2 | DISARM | Accusation audit, negative labels | Objections surfaced *before* the other side raised them |
| 3 | STEER | Calibrated questions, no defending | Questions asked before any number named |
| 4 | CLOSE | No-oriented questions, the six-second hold | Concessions made after silence |

One tool per week. The temptation to compress this into two weeks is strong and
you should resist it — the constraint is not how fast people can learn a
sentence, it's how long it takes for a sentence to survive a bad call.

**Wednesday and Thursday:** every rep records two live calls. Not their best
two — the next two. Self-selected tape is worthless.

**Friday, fifteen minutes:** score two calls in front of the room using Chapter
33's scorecard. Always your own or a volunteer's in week 1. Never start by
scoring your weakest rep in public; you will not get another volunteer for four
weeks.

## Scored on

The rollout itself is audited on four numbers, all readable from tape by someone
who wasn't in the room:

1. **Talk-time ratio, week 1 vs. week 4.** Target: under 40% by week 4.
   This is the headline number and the one to report upward.
2. **Drill completion.** Four of four Mondays. Three of four is a
   partial rollout and should be reported as such, not rounded up.
3. **Tape volume.** Two recorded calls per rep per week, weeks 1–4.
   Below 70% submission means the rollout is not actually running.
4. **Concessions after silence.** Count of times a rep improved their own
   offer with no counterparty request in between. Target: zero by week 4.

Number 4 is the one that pays for the program. A rep who stops bidding against
themselves in silence recovers the cost of any training on a single deal, and
it is trivially auditable from a recording.

## Failure mode

**How it goes wrong:** the manager runs weeks 1 and 2, gets visible enthusiasm,
declares it working, and stops. Adoption looks strong at day 14 and is gone by
day 30 — the week-3 dip arrives on schedule with no scheduled drill to absorb
it. This is by a wide margin the most common failure, and it is caused by the
rollout working early, not by it failing.

**What it looks like:** week 2 attendance is high and week 3 attendance is
"most people." Recordings taper. Someone suggests moving the Monday session to
"as needed."

**The recovery:** restart at week 3 rather than week 1. Do not re-teach labels —
the floor will read repetition as the program having no plan, and you'll lose
the room. Say plainly that weeks 3 and 4 were skipped and are now running, and
run them.

**The second failure mode** is scoring people instead of calls. The moment a
scorecard row is read as a verdict on a rep rather than a description of a
recording, tape submission collapses and everything downstream stops working.
Chapter 33 opens on exactly this problem, because it is the one that quietly
kills more rollouts than the week-3 dip does.

## Field Card — The 30-Day Rollout

```
COMMITMENT   15 min Mon · 2 recorded calls Wed/Thu · 15 min Fri · × 4 weeks

WEEK 1  READ    labels, silence          → talk-time ratio
WEEK 2  DISARM  audit, negative labels   → objections pre-surfaced
WEEK 3  STEER   calibrated questions     → questions before numbers
WEEK 4  CLOSE   no-oriented Qs, the hold → concessions after silence

MONDAY 15   2 min standard · 3 min demo (bad, then good)
            7 min pairs, 3 rounds, rotate · 3 min observers report NUMBERS

EXPECT      wk 1–2 awkward (good) · wk 3 dip (on schedule) · wk 4 unprompted use

AUDIT       talk-time <40% · 4/4 drills · ≥70% tape · 0 concessions-after-silence

DON'T       skip wk 3 · score people instead of calls · let reps pick the tape
```
