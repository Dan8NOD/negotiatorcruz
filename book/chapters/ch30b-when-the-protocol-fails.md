# Chapter 30: When the Protocol Fails

### Part 2 of 2 · The Practice

**Part V · APPLICATIONS**

*Part 1 of 2 covers the standard, when to reach for it, the words, and what good sounds like.*

## The drill

**"Kill the Deal."** Twenty-five minutes, run quarterly on live pipeline.

1. Each rep brings their **oldest open deal.**
2. They run all three diagnoses out loud: bad faith, no authority, deadlock, with evidence for each, not impressions.
3. Then the four questions above, answered honestly, in front of the room.
4. Then the hard part: **each rep names one deal they will kill this week**,
   and drafts the acknowledgement sentence they'll use.
5. Next session opens with what happened.

Step 4 is the drill and rooms resist it, which is the point. Most pipelines
carry deals that everyone privately knows are dead and nobody will say so,
because a dead deal removed from the forecast is a visible loss and a dead deal
left in it is an invisible one. This drill makes it visible on purpose.

---

## Scored on

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| **Diagnosis before escalation** | Any deal stalled 3+ conversations gets the three-condition check before more effort is spent. Binary. |
| **Small-commitment test** | Requested on every suspected bad-faith deal. Binary. |
| **Time to kill** | Days from first stall signal to a definite answer. Track it. This is the number that frees capacity. |
| **Clean endings** | Deals ended with an explicit acknowledgement rather than fading. Percentage. |
| **Reopen rate** | Of deals where "sounds like that's a no" was used, how many reopened. Usually surprises people. |

## Failure mode

**Trying harder.** The rep, correctly trained, responds to a stalling
negotiation by running the Protocol more carefully. More labels. Better
questions. A tighter summary.

In all three failure conditions this makes things worse, because sustained
attention from someone who cannot be helped, will not agree, or genuinely
cannot move reads as pressure regardless of how well executed it is. The
technique is fine. It is aimed at a problem it cannot solve.

**What it looks like:** effort rising while movement stays at zero across three
or more conversations.

**The recovery:** stop and diagnose before you spend anything else.

```
  "I've been working this like there's a version that
   lands, and I'm not sure there is. Is there a version
   of this that works for you, or should I stop?"
```

**The second failure mode: mistaking a hard negotiation for a failing one.** The
inverse error, and it's expensive in the other direction. A counterpart who is
difficult, slow, or unpleasant is not necessarily acting in bad faith. Chapter
28's disputes are often all three and entirely winnable. The three
conditions have specific tells. Apply the tests rather than the feeling. The
feeling is often just fatigue.
