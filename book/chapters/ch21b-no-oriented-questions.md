# Chapter 21: No-Oriented Questions

### Part 2 of 2 · The Practice

**Part IV · CLOSE**

*Part 1 of 2 covers the standard, when to reach for it, the words, and what good sounds like.*

---

## The drill

**"Ask and Absorb."** Fifteen minutes, pairs, recorded.

1. The operator delivers a closing ask in no-oriented form.
2. The counterpart's instruction, unknown to the operator, is to answer
   honestly, and roughly a third of the time, to say the hard version: *"Yes,
   actually, close the file."*
3. The operator must **respond without arguing.** Acknowledge, ask one
   calibrated question, and stop.
4. Score two things: was the question genuinely no-oriented, and did the
   operator absorb the no without negotiating against it?
5. Swap.

Step 3 is the drill. Anyone can ask a no-oriented question. The skill is what
you do in the two seconds after a real no, which is when most reps start
discounting.

---

## Scored on

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| **Closing question orientation** | The ask is no-oriented. Binary, and on most floors it starts near zero. |
| **Asked after "that's right"** | Close follows a confirmed summary, not preceding one. Binary. |
| **Silence held** | Full pause after the ask. No "because," no "or." |
| **Concessions after a no** | Zero. A no is information, not a trigger to discount. |
| **Stalled deals resolved** | Of deals over 60 days, how many got a definite answer after a file-closing question. Track quarterly. |

That last row is the commercial argument for the whole chapter. Stalled
pipeline is expensive in forecast accuracy long before it's expensive in
revenue, and this family is the cheapest instrument for clearing it.

---

## Failure mode

**Softening the ask into nothing.** The rep, at the moment of closing, cannot
tolerate the exposure and hedges the question until it can't be answered:
*"So I guess, whenever you're ready, if it makes sense, maybe we could think
about possibly getting something over to you?"*

Nothing was asked. Nothing will be answered. The meeting ends warmly and the
deal is exactly where it was.

**What it looks like on tape:** three or more qualifiers in the closing
sentence, and no question mark anywhere near a decision.

**The recovery:** ask it again, clean, and say why.

```
  "I buried that. Let me ask it properly, is there any
   reason we can't proceed?"
```

**The second failure mode: treating a no as a starting gun.** They say no, and
the rep, who has been trained that no means "not yet", immediately begins
improving the offer. This teaches the counterpart that refusal is profitable,
and it converts your close into an auction where you are the only bidder. A no
gets acknowledged and questioned. It does not get paid.
