# Chapter 18 — Trading: Never Give, Always Swap

## The drill

**"Currency Map."** Twenty-five minutes, run per deal or per account.

1. Draw two columns: **what we can give** and **what they can give.**
2. Fill both — at least eight items each, and **money may only appear once per
   column.** That constraint is the drill. It forces the non-cash inventory
   that most teams have never written down.
3. Score every item twice: what it costs the giver, what it's worth to the
   receiver. Cheap-to-give, expensive-to-receive items are your currency.
4. Rank your trades by that gap.
5. Each rep writes the exact sentence they'll use to propose their top trade.

Run it once per major account and keep the map. Most floors discover they have
six or seven genuine chips they've never used and have been paying in margin
for years because margin was the only instrument anyone had named.

---

## Scored on

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| **Unreciprocated concessions** | Zero. The core metric of the chapter and countable from any deal record. |
| **Non-cash trade ratio** | Percentage of concessions paid for with something other than price. Below 50% means the floor is one-dimensional. |
| **Trade named simultaneously** | The ask attached to the concession in the same breath, not raised later. Binary. |
| **Currency map exists** | Per major account. Binary. |
| **Asymmetry captured** | Did the trade obtained cost them less than the concession cost you? Scored per trade. |

The first row is the one that shows up in margin. Most floors do not know their
unreciprocated concession count, and on the ones that start counting it, the
number falls sharply within a month purely from being watched.

---

## Failure mode

**The goodwill concession.** The rep gives something away early — a discount, a
free month, expedited onboarding — reasoning that it builds trust and will be
remembered when it matters.

It will not be remembered. It will be **absorbed into the baseline**, and the
next conversation starts from the new position. Worse, you have demonstrated
that your prices have a soft layer, and every subsequent number you say is now
heard as a first offer.

**What it looks like on tape:** a concession with no "if" in the sentence.

**The recovery:** convert it retroactively. Late is worse than simultaneous but
far better than never.

```
  "I moved on the implementation fee earlier without asking
   for anything, which wasn't fair to my side. Can we put
   the start date at the first of the month?"
```

Naming your own error as the reason makes the ask difficult to refuse, because
refusing means insisting on keeping something they were given for free.

**The second failure mode: trading things that aren't yours.** Reps under
pressure trade delivery dates the delivery team hasn't agreed to, support terms
support hasn't seen, and customizations engineering will refuse. That isn't a
trade, it's a promise written against someone else's account, and it comes due
during implementation — which is where renewals are actually decided. Know your
own inventory before you go shopping in it.
