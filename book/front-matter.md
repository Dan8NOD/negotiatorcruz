## Page 1: Title

> # The Cruz Protocol
> ### A Field Manual for Commercial Negotiation
>
> **Dan Cruz**
> Negotiators on Demand · Chicago

**Positioning note, not for print.** The subtitle does one job: it tells a
buyer what category this is before they read a word. *Field manual* is the
load-bearing phrase. Not a guide, which sounds optional. Not a journey, which
sounds like a memoir. Not secrets or playbook, which sound like a webinar. A
manual is a thing an organization can adopt, and adoption is what gets a
budget line.

## Page 3: Who this manual is for

> the job.

> **Real estate principals and brokers.** Renewals, price increases, and
> counterparts who have done this more times than you have.
>
> **Enablement and L&D.** Start at Part VI. It is the installation section,

> **And anyone who has walked out of a negotiation knowing they lost it

**Note, not for print.** Naming job titles explicitly is the highest-yield
page in the front matter. A reader who finds their own title on page three has
already decided the book is for them, and the corporate buyer skimming for
relevance stops skimming.

## Page 4: The standard this is written to

> The tools in this manual work on everyone. That includes people who trust
> you, people who like you, and people who are not equipped to notice.
>
> That fact is the reason the standard exists rather than a reason to be coy
> about it:
>
> **These tools are used to create clarity, not confusion. More dignity, not
> less. To listen to understand, not to win. When the
> conversation ends, the other side should not feel beaten. They should feel
> heard. That is the standard.**
>
> This is not softness and it is not a disclaimer. It is the commercial
> argument. A negotiation that leaves the other side feeling handled produces
> a signed contract and a counterpart who spends the next twelve months
> looking for the exit. Every renewal in your pipeline is a bill that arrives
> for how the first negotiation was conducted.
>
> Any technique that requires the other side not to notice is outside this
> standard. If you find one in here, it is an error and it should be reported.

## How to use this manual

*Chapter 3 carries this in full. The front matter version is four lines and a
pointer, because the reader has not bought anything yet and a full operating
manual on page six is a wall.*

> Three ways in, depending on who you are.
>
> **Operator:** Part 0, then Part I. Stop there and run the drills for two
> weeks before you read further.
> **Installing this on a team:** Part 0, then jump to Part VI. It tells you
> what to measure, and what you measure on day one decides what you get on
> day ninety.
> **Deciding whether to spend money on this:** Chapter 2, Chapter 30, Chapter
> 33. In that order, nothing else. Chapter 30 is where the method fails.
>
> Chapter 3 explains all three in full.
